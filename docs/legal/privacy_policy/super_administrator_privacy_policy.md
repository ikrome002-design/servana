## 3. DESCRIPTION OF SERVANA

3.1. Servana is a multi-tenant SaaS web platform for service-based SMEs, including barbershops, salons, massage parlours, spas, grooming studios, beauty parlours, and similar appointment or walk-in businesses. Servana is designed to operate as a service-operations platform, not merely as a booking application, point-of-sale tool, or invoicing utility.

3.2. Servana enables Customers to digitise, control, and audit day-to-day operations, including merchant onboarding, merchant tenant creation, merchant branches, staff and personnel access, front-office operations, client records, walk-ins, appointments, queue management, service delivery tracking, preferred or next-available personnel selection, invoice generation, offline payment recording, receipt generation, personnel commission visibility, client data protection and contact access controls, platform fee calculation, service fee tier invoice pricing, merchant financial visibility, and audit-ready operational and financial records.

3.3. Servana operates on a multi-tenant principle. Each merchant operates as an isolated tenant. A merchant’s data must not be accessible, inferable, editable, exportable, or enumerable by another merchant, except through controlled, authorised, platform-level Citrus Labs Limited workflows where Citrus Labs Limited has lawful authority and operational necessity to support, secure, administer, audit, investigate, comply with law, enforce rights, or operate Servana.

3.4. Servana records offline or off-platform payments. Servana may record payment method, amount, reference, status, validation details, cash-up records, disputes, external refund records, receipts, invoices, and related audit trails, but Servana is not represented by this Policy as directly processing client-to-merchant payments inside the platform unless a payment-processing feature is expressly enabled and disclosed through applicable terms, notices, or Customer configuration.

3.5. Servana uses Magic Link authentication for platform users where configured according to the product scope. Authentication may include email verification, one-time-use tokens, token expiry, role status checks, account status checks, suspension status checks, tenant checks, branch-scope checks, session controls, rate limiting, login audit logs, and optional additional authentication controls for high-privilege roles.
