## 1.4 Does Servana process client payments?

No. Servana does not process client-to-merchant payments inside the platform.

Servana records offline payments so your business can track what was paid, how it was paid, who recorded it, who validated it, and which invoice or receipt it relates to.

Offline payment methods may include:

* Cash
* M-Pesa
* Bank transfer
* Card terminal
* Voucher
* Split payment
* Other merchant-defined offline payment methods

---

## 1.5 What is the difference between payment recording and payment processing?

Payment processing means money moves through the platform.

Payment recording means your team enters details of a payment that happened outside the platform.

Servana records payments. It helps your business keep proper payment records, but the actual money movement happens outside Servana.

A merchant tenant is your business’s separate workspace inside Servana.

Your merchant tenant contains your branches, staff, clients, services, invoices, payments, receipts, reports, and audit records.

Each merchant tenant is kept separate from other merchants. Your business data should not be visible to another merchant.

# 2. Merchant Administrator Account

## 2.1 What is a Merchant Administrator?

The Merchant Administrator is the main owner or authorized operator of a merchant business account in Servana.

This user is responsible for creating the merchant account, completing the first setup steps, creating branches, adding initial staff emails, viewing merchant-level reports, and managing high-level merchant activity.

In Servana, the **Merchant Administrator** and **Merchant Owner** are the same account type.

---

## 2.2 Who should be the Merchant Administrator?

The Merchant Administrator should be the person who has authority to manage the business account.

This may be:

* The business owner
* A director
* A manager
* An authorized operator
* A senior administrator trusted to manage the merchant account

This role should not be given casually. It has important visibility over branches, staff activity, reports, platform fee records, and merchant setup.

---

## 2.3 Does the Super Administrator create my merchant account?

No. The Merchant Administrator self-registers the business account.

After registration:

1. The system creates the merchant tenant.
2. The registering user becomes the Merchant Owner / Merchant Administrator.
3. The Merchant Administrator completes first-time setup.
4. The Merchant Administrator is then redirected to the Merchant Administrator dashboard.

The Super Administrator does not create the first Merchant Administrator.

---

## 2.4 Does the Super Administrator approve my dashboard access before I start?

No. Merchant Administrator dashboard access is not held for manual Super Administrator approval.

After self-registration, your next step is to complete the required first-time setup.

---

## 2.5 What can I do as a Merchant Administrator?

As a Merchant Administrator, you can:

* Self-register the merchant business account.
* Complete and manage the merchant profile.
* Select the merchant service fee tier where available.
* Create Merchant Branch accounts.
* Complete and manage Merchant Branch profiles.
* Add initial Merchant Branch account user emails.
* Add initial Merchant Human Resource account user emails.
* View all branches under your merchant account.
* View staff under your branches.
* View branch revenue performance.
* View service performance.
* View staff performance.
* View merchant-level platform fee records.
* Receive daily branch day-close reports.
* Receive daily branch cash-up and reconciliation reports.
* Suspend inactive or abusive merchant users where the platform allows it.
* Preserve visibility over historical records and audit activity.

---

## 2.6 What can I not do as a Merchant Administrator?

The Merchant Administrator role is powerful, but it is not meant to do everything.

As a Merchant Administrator, you do not directly:

* Configure services or service prices at Merchant Administrator level.
* Assign personnel to branches.
* Configure personnel service eligibility.
* Configure Merchant Personnel commissions directly.
* Link Merchant Personnel to accounts.
* Perform normal Front Office workflows.
* Validate payments as the default Finance workflow.
* Run daily queue operations as a branch user.
* Create all staff account types directly beyond the initial setup roles.

These boundaries keep each role clear and help reduce mistakes.

---

## 2.7 Why does Servana limit what the Merchant Administrator can do?

Servana separates responsibilities so that the business has cleaner controls.

For example:

* Branch users manage branch services and branch operations.
* HR users manage staff identity, branch assignment, service eligibility, and availability.
* Finance users validate offline payments and manage financial records.
* Front Office users handle daily client-facing workflows.
* Personnel users focus on their own service work.
* Audit users review activity without changing records.

This keeps your business organized and makes audit records more useful.

# 3. Registration and First-Time Setup

## 3.1 How do I create a merchant account?

You create a merchant account by registering as the Merchant Administrator.

After registration, Servana creates your merchant tenant and assigns you as the Merchant Owner / Merchant Administrator.

---

## 3.2 What happens after I register?

After registration, you complete first-time setup before entering the Merchant Administrator dashboard.

The setup includes:

1. Selecting a service fee tier.
2. Completing your merchant profile.
3. Creating at least one branch.
4. Completing the branch profile.
5. Adding initial staff emails for the Merchant Branch account user and Merchant Human Resource account user.
6. Selecting the branch of operation for those initial staff users.
7. Triggering welcome emails for Magic Link login.
8. Entering the Merchant Administrator dashboard.

---

## 3.3 Why must I complete first-time setup?

First-time setup gives your merchant account a proper operating structure.

Without it, the platform would not know:

* Which business is using Servana
* Which branch exists first
* Which branch user should manage that branch
* Which HR user should manage staff for that branch
* Which service fee tier applies to your merchant-client invoices
* Where branch reports and activity should be organized

The setup keeps your account clean from the beginning.

---

## 3.4 Can I skip branch creation during setup?

No. At least one branch is needed before the merchant account can operate properly.

A branch represents a physical or operational location, such as:

* Westlands Branch
* Kilimani Salon
* CBD Barbershop
* Hotel Spa Branch
* Mobile Service Unit

Even a single-location business should create one branch.

---

## 3.5 What happens when I only have one branch?

Where only one branch exists, Servana can auto-select that branch when you add initial staff emails during setup.

This makes onboarding faster and avoids unnecessary branch-selection steps.

---

## 3.6 Which staff emails do I add during first-time setup?

During first-time setup, the Merchant Administrator adds initial email addresses for:

* Merchant Branch account user
* Merchant Human Resource account user

These users receive welcome emails explaining how to log in using Magic Link.

---

## 3.7 Can I add Front Office, Finance, Audit, or Personnel users during first-time setup?

No. Those users are added through the Merchant Human Resource workflow after setup.

The Merchant Human Resource user manages staff creation within the assigned branch scope.

This keeps staff setup controlled and organized.

---

# 4. Accounts and Login

## 4.1 How do users log in to Servana?

Users log in through Magic Link sent to their email address.

The user requests access, receives a link by email, and clicks the link to log in.

---

## 4.2 Can a user log in just because their email exists?

No. Servana checks more than the email address.

Before allowing login, the system checks that:

* The email exists.
* The user belongs to the correct merchant.
* The account is active.
* The role is active.
* The user is not suspended.
* The user is assigned to the correct branch where branch access applies.
* The Magic Link is valid, unused, and not expired.

This protects your account from accidental or unauthorized access.

---

## 4.3 What should I do if my Magic Link does not work?

Check the following:

* The link may have expired.
* The link may already have been used.
* You may be checking the wrong email inbox.
* Your account may not be active.
* Your role may not be active.
* Your browser may be blocking the login flow.
* Your email client may be opening the link incorrectly.

Request a new Magic Link. If the issue continues, contact support through the Help & Support workflow.

---

## 4.4 Can users share Magic Links?

No. Magic Links should not be shared.

Each user should log in through their own email address. Sharing links weakens security and makes activity records unclear.

---

## 4.5 What happens if a staff member leaves the business?

The appropriate administrator should suspend, deactivate, or remove the user’s access promptly.

The platform should preserve historical records connected to that user, such as invoices, service sessions, payment records, receipts, role changes, and audit activity.

This protects business history while preventing future access.

---

## 4.6 Can one person have multiple roles?

Yes, where the platform allows it and the business chooses to set it up that way.

However, role combinations should be handled carefully. Too much access in one account can increase the risk of mistakes, weak controls, or unclear accountability.

# 6. Branch Management

## 6.1 What is a branch in Servana?

A branch is a physical or operational business location under your merchant account.

Examples include:

* Kilimani Branch
* Westlands Branch
* CBD Branch
* Hotel Spa Branch
* Mobile Grooming Unit

Each branch has its own operating profile, staff scope, services, pricing, queues, appointments, invoices, payment records, reports, and audit logs.

---

## 6.2 Who creates branches?

The Merchant Administrator creates Merchant Branch accounts.

Branch users do not create branches.

---

## 6.3 What information is needed for a branch profile?

A branch profile should include:

* Branch name
* Branch code
* Physical address
* Town, city, or area
* Branch phone number
* Branch email, where available
* Business category, where needed
* Branch status

These details help with reporting, filtering, invoicing, support, and day-to-day operations.

---

## 6.4 What is a branch code?

A branch code is a short identifier for a branch.

For example:

* KIL-001 for Kilimani
* WES-001 for Westlands
* CBD-001 for CBD

Branch codes make reports, invoices, receipts, and support references easier to read.

---

## 6.5 Can I manage more than one branch?

Yes. The Merchant Administrator can view and manage branches created under the merchant account.

Each branch should still have its own branch user and staff structure so daily operations remain clear.

---

## 6.6 Can a branch user manage another branch?

No. A Merchant Branch account user manages only the branch they are assigned to.

This keeps branch activity separate and prevents users from accidentally changing the wrong branch.

---

## 6.7 Can I suspend a branch?

Yes, where the platform allows it.

A branch may be suspended for reasons such as inactivity, business decision, billing issue, internal review, or platform-policy issue.

A suspended branch may be blocked from accepting new walk-ins, appointments, queues, invoices, payments, and service sessions, depending on configuration.

---

## 6.8 Can I close or archive a branch?

Yes, where the platform allows it, but branch closure should be handled carefully.

A branch should not be closed or archived while live operational records still exist.

Examples of records that should be resolved first include:

* Active queue entries
* In-progress service sessions
* Unpaid invoices
* Pending payment validations
* Unissued receipts for validated payments
* Pending appointment check-ins
* Unclosed branch day
* Unresolved cash-up discrepancies

---

## 6.9 Why does Servana protect branch closure?

Branch closure affects records, reports, payments, and customer history.

Servana should prevent closure from breaking active workflows or leaving unfinished activity behind.

This protects the business, the staff, the customer experience, and the audit trail.

## 9. Client Pricing, Preferred Personnel Fees, Invoices, and Payment Records

### 9.1 How does Servana help me keep client pricing clear?

Servana helps your business keep service pricing, client invoices, payment records, and receipts easier to follow.

As a Merchant Administrator, you should not have to guess what happened at a branch, search through scattered notes, or depend only on verbal updates. Servana gives your business a cleaner way to see what was charged, what was recorded, what still needs attention, and what has already been completed.

The goal is simple: when a client receives a service, your team should have a clear record of the service, the price, the payment status, and the receipt trail.

### 9.2 Who sets service prices in Servana?

Service prices are managed at branch level by the authorized branch user.

This keeps pricing close to the team responsible for day-to-day branch operations. A branch may have its own services, prices, durations, add-ons, or operating style, especially where different locations serve different client needs.

As Merchant Administrator, you get visibility across the business without needing to personally handle every service setup action yourself.

### 9.3 Can every user change service prices?

No. Service prices should only be changed by users with the right branch-level permission.

This protects the business from random, accidental, or unauthorized price changes. It also helps your team avoid situations where users outside the correct role change pricing without the right responsibility.

The right people set the prices. The rest of the team uses those prices during the correct workflow.

### 9.4 What is a preferred personnel fee?

A preferred personnel fee is an extra amount that may apply when a client chooses to wait for a specific staff member instead of taking the next available person.

This is useful for barbershops, salons, spas, grooming studios, massage studios, beauty parlours, and similar service businesses where clients often prefer a specific barber, stylist, therapist, beautician, nail technician, or specialist.

The choice stays simple for the client: take the next available person, or choose a specific person where that option is available.

### 9.5 Who sets the preferred personnel fee?

The preferred personnel fee is set by the Merchant Human Resource account user for the relevant branch.

This keeps the fee connected to the people who manage branch staff setup, service eligibility, personnel availability, and staff-related configuration.

It also keeps the front desk experience clean. Front Office users can present the option to the client, but they do not casually change the fee during daily service.

### 9.6 Why is the preferred personnel fee handled by the Merchant Human Resource account user?

Because the fee is tied to personnel, not just to a service.

A client is not only choosing a haircut, massage, treatment, styling session, or grooming service. The client is choosing a specific person to provide that service.

The Merchant Human Resource account user is the right role to manage that setup because HR already handles staff structure, service eligibility, availability, and branch-scoped personnel details.

This keeps the fee organized, branch-specific, and easier for the business to control.

### 9.7 Can each branch have its own preferred personnel fee setup?

Yes. The preferred personnel fee is handled at branch level.

That means one branch can have its own preferred personnel fee setup, while another branch can have a different setup where the platform allows it.

This matters because branches are not always identical. A busy flagship branch, a smaller neighborhood branch, or a specialist branch may not operate the same way. Servana keeps the structure flexible enough for each branch to reflect its own working reality.

### 9.8 Can Front Office users change the preferred personnel fee while serving a client?

No. Front Office users should not change the preferred personnel fee while serving a client.

Their role is to guide the client through the available options, select the service, show the preferred personnel choice where available, confirm the client’s decision, create the correct service flow, and record the payment details through the allowed process.

This keeps the front desk fast, simple, and consistent.

### 9.9 Can Merchant Personnel users change their own preferred personnel fee?

No. Merchant Personnel users should not set or change their own preferred personnel fee.

Personnel users focus on their assigned clients, appointments, queue, services, and own performance visibility. Allowing each staff member to change their own fee would create confusion, inconsistency, and possible disputes.

The fee is managed through the branch’s Merchant Human Resource account user so the business keeps control over how preferred personnel choices are priced.

### 9.10 Can the Merchant Administrator view preferred personnel fee activity?

Yes. As Merchant Administrator, you may be able to see preferred personnel fee activity through the relevant reports, invoice records, branch activity, and business visibility areas.

You do not need to manage every preferred personnel choice manually. Servana is designed to help you see the bigger picture: which services are moving, which staff are being requested, which branches are active, and how those choices affect business performance.

### 9.11 Will the preferred personnel fee be shown before the client confirms?

Yes. Where the preferred personnel feature is available, the fee should be visible before the client confirms the choice.

This matters because clients should not be surprised later. If a client chooses a specific staff member and that choice carries an extra fee, the client should see that fee before the transaction moves forward.

Clear pricing helps the branch avoid awkward conversations, payment disputes, and confusion at receipt stage.

### 9.12 Will the preferred personnel fee appear on the client invoice?

Yes. Where the preferred personnel fee applies, it should appear clearly on the invoice as a separate service-related line item.

This keeps the invoice easy to explain. The client can see the service selected, the service price, the preferred personnel fee where applicable, and the final amount due.

Good invoices do not hide important details. They help everyone understand what was agreed.

### 9.13 Does the preferred personnel fee count as merchant revenue?

Yes, where the preferred personnel fee is part of the merchant-client service flow, it should be treated as merchant revenue.

The client is paying the merchant for the choice to wait for or receive service from a specific staff member. Servana keeps that activity visible in the right places so the business can understand what clients are choosing and how that affects branch performance.

### 9.14 Does the preferred personnel fee automatically mean the staff member earns extra commission?

Not automatically.

Commission treatment depends on how the branch and HR setup handle personnel commission rules. A preferred personnel fee may be visible in the invoice and business records, but whether it affects a staff member’s commission should follow the configured personnel and commission setup.

This helps avoid assumptions. The amount shown to the client and the commission earned by staff are connected business topics, but they are not always the same thing.

### 9.15 Can a preferred personnel choice be changed after the client confirms?

Yes, but only through the proper workflow.

Sometimes the selected staff member may become unavailable, the client may decide not to wait, the branch may need to reassign the service, or the session may need to be cancelled or adjusted.

When that happens, Servana should keep the change traceable. The right user should give a reason, the record should remain clear, and any invoice or fee adjustment should follow the proper process.

This protects your branch from confusion later.

### 9.16 What happens if the preferred staff member becomes unavailable?

If the preferred staff member becomes unavailable, the branch may need to handle the situation through options such as waiting longer, reassigning the client, cancelling the session, or adjusting the fee where appropriate.

The important point is that the change should not happen casually or silently. It should be handled in a way that keeps the client record, queue record, invoice record, and payment trail understandable.

Servana is designed to support that kind of operational clarity.

### 9.17 Can the preferred personnel fee be reversed or adjusted?

Yes, where the right workflow allows it.

For example, if a client paid or agreed to wait for a specific staff member but that staff member later became unavailable, the branch may need to adjust the invoice or reverse the preferred personnel fee.

That adjustment should be recorded properly. It should not be hidden, deleted, or handled outside the system without a clear trail.

### 9.18 Why does Servana separate service pricing from preferred personnel fees?

Because they represent different things.

Service pricing is the price of the actual service, such as a haircut, massage, manicure, styling session, grooming service, or beauty treatment.

A preferred personnel fee is connected to the client choosing a specific staff member.

Keeping them separate makes the invoice easier to explain and gives your business better visibility into what clients are paying for.

### 9.19 Why does Servana separate pricing from payment recording?

Because pricing and payment are not the same thing.

Pricing explains what the client is being charged. Payment recording shows what the client paid, how they paid, when it was recorded, and what reference or proof was captured.

Servana keeps those steps connected, but not blurred together. This gives your business cleaner records and helps Finance users validate payments more confidently.

### 9.20 Does Servana process client payments?

No. Servana records offline payments; it does not process client payments inside the platform at launch.

Your clients may pay your business through cash, M-Pesa, bank transfer, card terminal, voucher, split payment, or another supported offline method. Servana helps your team record those payment details so your business has a reliable trail.

This gives you structure without forcing your business to change how clients already pay you.

### 9.21 Who records client payment details?

Client payment details may be recorded by the authorized user in the branch workflow, depending on the permissions configured for the account.

In daily operations, this may be handled by Front Office or Finance users, depending on how the merchant has set up responsibilities.

The important point is that payment recording should happen through the proper workflow, not through informal messages, memory, or untracked notes.

### 9.22 Why does payment validation matter?

Payment validation helps protect your business.

A payment can be recorded quickly, but that does not always mean it has been confirmed. Finance validation helps your team check the amount, payment method, reference, and status before the record is treated as complete.

This reduces the risk of duplicate references, wrong payment amounts, early receipts, missing proof, and end-of-day confusion.

### 9.23 When is a receipt generated?

A receipt should be generated only after the right payment validation steps are complete.

That keeps your receipts more reliable. It also helps avoid situations where a client receives a receipt before the payment record has been properly checked.

For a Merchant Administrator, this creates a cleaner trail from service to invoice, from invoice to payment, and from validated payment to receipt.

### 9.24 Can invoices or receipts be deleted when there is a mistake?

Servana is designed to protect important financial records instead of allowing them to disappear casually.

If something needs to be corrected, the proper workflow should preserve the record, show what changed, and keep the history visible where the right user has permission.

That approach helps your business stay organized and reduces disputes when reviewing past activity.

### 9.25 Why does Servana keep invoice and receipt records traceable?

Because service businesses need trust in their numbers.

The authorized support person opens the Help & Support page, clicks **Contact Support**, completes the structured form, and submits it. The system prepares an email to the support team with the submitted details.

---

## 21.2 Who can contact support?

Account-specific support is handled through the Authorized Support Contact.

For a merchant account, this may be the Merchant Administrator or another designated account role approved for support communication.

---

## 21.3 Can every user send account support requests?

No. General help information may be visible to users, but account-specific support should go through the Authorized Support Contact.

This keeps support clear, secure, and easier to manage.

---

## 21.4 What is on the Help & Support page?

The Help & Support page may include:

* Basic FAQs
* Help information
* Self-service guidance
* A structured support request form visible to the Authorized Support Contact

The page is intentionally simple.

---

## 21.5 Is there live chat?

No standard live chat is required.

Servana uses a lean form-to-email support workflow unless another support channel is specifically enabled.

---

## 21.6 Is there phone support?

No standard phone support is required.

Support is handled through email-based follow-up unless Citrus Labs Limited provides another channel for a specific case.

---

## 21.7 Is there a ticketing dashboard?

No ticketing dashboard is required for the basic support workflow.

The standard process is a simple support form that prepares an email to the support team.

---

## 21.8 What information should I include in a support request?

Include:

* Merchant name
* Branch name, where relevant
* Affected user’s direct email address
* Description of the issue
* Feature or module affected
* Invoice, receipt, payment, appointment, queue, or record reference where available
* Error message, where available
* Date and time of the issue
* Browser and device details, where helpful
* Screenshot, where helpful
* Confirmation that you are authorized to share the information

Clear details help the support team respond faster.

---

## 21.9 Who does support follow up with?

Support may follow up by email with:

* The Authorized Support Contact
* The affected user, where appropriate
* Both, where the issue requires it

---

## 21.10 Why does support use the Authorized Support Contact?

This helps protect your account.

It ensures support requests come from someone trusted by the merchant account and helps avoid confusion when sensitive account, staff, client, invoice, payment, or access details are involved.
