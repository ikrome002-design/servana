## 1.4 Does Servana process client payments?

No. Servana does not process client-to-merchant payments inside the platform.

Servana records offline payments so your business can track what was paid, how it was paid, who recorded it, who validated it, and which invoice or receipt it relates to.

Offline payment methods may include:

* Cash
* M-Pesa
* Bank transfer
* Card terminal
* Voucher
* Split payment
* Other merchant-defined offline payment methods

---

## 1.5 What is the difference between payment recording and payment processing?

Payment processing means money moves through the platform.

Payment recording means your team enters details of a payment that happened outside the platform.

Servana records payments. It helps your business keep proper payment records, but the actual money movement happens outside Servana.

A merchant tenant is your business’s separate workspace inside Servana.

Your merchant tenant contains your branches, staff, clients, services, invoices, payments, receipts, reports, and audit records.

Each merchant tenant is kept separate from other merchants. Your business data should not be visible to another merchant.

# 2. Merchant Administrator Account

## 2.1 What is a Merchant Administrator?

The Merchant Administrator is the main owner or authorized operator of a merchant business account in Servana.

This user is responsible for creating the merchant account, completing the first setup steps, creating branches, adding initial staff emails, viewing merchant-level reports, and managing high-level merchant activity.

In Servana, the **Merchant Administrator** and **Merchant Owner** are the same account type.

---

## 2.2 Who should be the Merchant Administrator?

The Merchant Administrator should be the person who has authority to manage the business account.

This may be:

* The business owner
* A director
* A manager
* An authorized operator
* A senior administrator trusted to manage the merchant account

This role should not be given casually. It has important visibility over branches, staff activity, reports, platform fee records, and merchant setup.

---

## 2.3 Does the Super Administrator create my merchant account?

No. The Merchant Administrator self-registers the business account.

After registration:

1. The system creates the merchant tenant.
2. The registering user becomes the Merchant Owner / Merchant Administrator.
3. The Merchant Administrator completes first-time setup.
4. The Merchant Administrator is then redirected to the Merchant Administrator dashboard.

The Super Administrator does not create the first Merchant Administrator.

---

## 2.4 Does the Super Administrator approve my dashboard access before I start?

No. Merchant Administrator dashboard access is not held for manual Super Administrator approval.

After self-registration, your next step is to complete the required first-time setup.

---

## 2.5 What can I do as a Merchant Administrator?

As a Merchant Administrator, you can:

* Self-register the merchant business account.
* Complete and manage the merchant profile.
* Select the merchant service fee tier where available.
* Create Merchant Branch accounts.
* Complete and manage Merchant Branch profiles.
* Add initial Merchant Branch account user emails.
* Add initial Merchant Human Resource account user emails.
* View all branches under your merchant account.
* View staff under your branches.
* View branch revenue performance.
* View service performance.
* View staff performance.
* View merchant-level platform fee records.
* Receive daily branch day-close reports.
* Receive daily branch cash-up and reconciliation reports.
* Suspend inactive or abusive merchant users where the platform allows it.
* Preserve visibility over historical records and audit activity.

---

## 2.6 What can I not do as a Merchant Administrator?

The Merchant Administrator role is powerful, but it is not meant to do everything.

As a Merchant Administrator, you do not directly:

* Configure services or service prices at Merchant Administrator level.
* Assign personnel to branches.
* Configure personnel service eligibility.
* Configure Merchant Personnel commissions directly.
* Link Merchant Personnel to accounts.
* Perform normal Front Office workflows.
* Validate payments as the default Finance workflow.
* Run daily queue operations as a branch user.
* Create all staff account types directly beyond the initial setup roles.

These boundaries keep each role clear and help reduce mistakes.

---

## 2.7 Why does Servana limit what the Merchant Administrator can do?

Servana separates responsibilities so that the business has cleaner controls.

For example:

* Branch users manage branch services and branch operations.
* HR users manage staff identity, branch assignment, service eligibility, and availability.
* Finance users validate offline payments and manage financial records.
* Front Office users handle daily client-facing workflows.
* Personnel users focus on their own service work.
* Audit users review activity without changing records.

This keeps your business organized and makes audit records more useful.

# 3. Registration and First-Time Setup

## 3.1 How do I create a merchant account?

You create a merchant account by registering as the Merchant Administrator.

After registration, Servana creates your merchant tenant and assigns you as the Merchant Owner / Merchant Administrator.

---

## 3.2 What happens after I register?

After registration, you complete first-time setup before entering the Merchant Administrator dashboard.

The setup includes:

1. Selecting a service fee tier.
2. Completing your merchant profile.
3. Creating at least one branch.
4. Completing the branch profile.
5. Adding initial staff emails for the Merchant Branch account user and Merchant Human Resource account user.
6. Selecting the branch of operation for those initial staff users.
7. Triggering welcome emails for Magic Link login.
8. Entering the Merchant Administrator dashboard.

---

## 3.3 Why must I complete first-time setup?

First-time setup gives your merchant account a proper operating structure.

Without it, the platform would not know:

* Which business is using Servana
* Which branch exists first
* Which branch user should manage that branch
* Which HR user should manage staff for that branch
* Which service fee tier applies to your merchant-client invoices
* Where branch reports and activity should be organized

The setup keeps your account clean from the beginning.

---

## 3.4 Can I skip branch creation during setup?

No. At least one branch is needed before the merchant account can operate properly.

A branch represents a physical or operational location, such as:

* Westlands Branch
* Kilimani Salon
* CBD Barbershop
* Hotel Spa Branch
* Mobile Service Unit

Even a single-location business should create one branch.

---

## 3.5 What happens when I only have one branch?

Where only one branch exists, Servana can auto-select that branch when you add initial staff emails during setup.

This makes onboarding faster and avoids unnecessary branch-selection steps.

---

## 3.6 Which staff emails do I add during first-time setup?

During first-time setup, the Merchant Administrator adds initial email addresses for:

* Merchant Branch account user
* Merchant Human Resource account user

These users receive welcome emails explaining how to log in using Magic Link.

---

## 3.7 Can I add Front Office, Finance, Audit, or Personnel users during first-time setup?

No. Those users are added through the Merchant Human Resource workflow after setup.

The Merchant Human Resource user manages staff creation within the assigned branch scope.

This keeps staff setup controlled and organized.

---

# 4. Accounts and Login

## 4.1 How do users log in to Servana?

Users log in through Magic Link sent to their email address.

The user requests access, receives a link by email, and clicks the link to log in.

---

## 4.2 Can a user log in just because their email exists?

No. Servana checks more than the email address.

Before allowing login, the system checks that:

* The email exists.
* The user belongs to the correct merchant.
* The account is active.
* The role is active.
* The user is not suspended.
* The user is assigned to the correct branch where branch access applies.
* The Magic Link is valid, unused, and not expired.

This protects your account from accidental or unauthorized access.

---

## 4.3 What should I do if my Magic Link does not work?

Check the following:

* The link may have expired.
* The link may already have been used.
* You may be checking the wrong email inbox.
* Your account may not be active.
* Your role may not be active.
* Your browser may be blocking the login flow.
* Your email client may be opening the link incorrectly.

Request a new Magic Link. If the issue continues, contact support through the Help & Support workflow.

---

## 4.4 Can users share Magic Links?

No. Magic Links should not be shared.

Each user should log in through their own email address. Sharing links weakens security and makes activity records unclear.

---

## 4.5 What happens if a staff member leaves the business?

The appropriate administrator should suspend, deactivate, or remove the user’s access promptly.

The platform should preserve historical records connected to that user, such as invoices, service sessions, payment records, receipts, role changes, and audit activity.

This protects business history while preventing future access.

---

## 4.6 Can one person have multiple roles?

Yes, where the platform allows it and the business chooses to set it up that way.

However, role combinations should be handled carefully. Too much access in one account can increase the risk of mistakes, weak controls, or unclear accountability.

# 6. Branch Management

## 6.1 What is a branch in Servana?

A branch is a physical or operational business location under your merchant account.

Examples include:

* Kilimani Branch
* Westlands Branch
* CBD Branch
* Hotel Spa Branch
* Mobile Grooming Unit

Each branch has its own operating profile, staff scope, services, pricing, queues, appointments, invoices, payment records, reports, and audit logs.

---

## 6.2 Who creates branches?

The Merchant Administrator creates Merchant Branch accounts.

Branch users do not create branches.

---

## 6.3 What information is needed for a branch profile?

A branch profile should include:

* Branch name
* Branch code
* Physical address
* Town, city, or area
* Branch phone number
* Branch email, where available
* Business category, where needed
* Branch status

These details help with reporting, filtering, invoicing, support, and day-to-day operations.

---

## 6.4 What is a branch code?

A branch code is a short identifier for a branch.

For example:

* KIL-001 for Kilimani
* WES-001 for Westlands
* CBD-001 for CBD

Branch codes make reports, invoices, receipts, and support references easier to read.

---

## 6.5 Can I manage more than one branch?

Yes. The Merchant Administrator can view and manage branches created under the merchant account.

Each branch should still have its own branch user and staff structure so daily operations remain clear.

---

## 6.6 Can a branch user manage another branch?

No. A Merchant Branch account user manages only the branch they are assigned to.

This keeps branch activity separate and prevents users from accidentally changing the wrong branch.

---

## 6.7 Can I suspend a branch?

Yes, where the platform allows it.

A branch may be suspended for reasons such as inactivity, business decision, billing issue, internal review, or platform-policy issue.

A suspended branch may be blocked from accepting new walk-ins, appointments, queues, invoices, payments, and service sessions, depending on configuration.

---

## 6.8 Can I close or archive a branch?

Yes, where the platform allows it, but branch closure should be handled carefully.

A branch should not be closed or archived while live operational records still exist.

Examples of records that should be resolved first include:

* Active queue entries
* In-progress service sessions
* Unpaid invoices
* Pending payment validations
* Unissued receipts for validated payments
* Pending appointment check-ins
* Unclosed branch day
* Unresolved cash-up discrepancies

---

## 6.9 Why does Servana protect branch closure?

Branch closure affects records, reports, payments, and customer history.

Servana should prevent closure from breaking active workflows or leaving unfinished activity behind.

This protects the business, the staff, the customer experience, and the audit trail.

# 9. Service Fee Tiers and Platform Fees

## 9.1 What is a service fee tier?

A service fee tier controls how the platform service fee affects the merchant-client invoice amount.

It affects what the client sees on the merchant-client invoice. It does not remove the merchant’s platform fee responsibility.

---

## 9.2 What service fee tiers are available?

Servana may support:

* Customer Centric
* Split Tier
* Business Centric

The available options depend on platform configuration.

---

## 9.3 What does Customer Centric mean?

Customer Centric means the client pays only the merchant’s service price.

Example:

* Service price: KES 500
* Platform service fee: KES 70
* Client invoice amount: KES 500
* Merchant platform fee responsibility: KES 70

The merchant absorbs the platform fee.

---

## 9.4 What does Split Tier mean?

Split Tier means the client invoice includes part of the platform service fee.

Example:

* Service price: KES 500
* Platform service fee: KES 70
* Client invoice amount: KES 535
* Merchant platform fee responsibility: KES 70

The client invoice includes 50% of the platform fee. The merchant still remains responsible for the full platform fee.

---

## 9.5 What does Business Centric mean?

Business Centric means the client invoice includes the full platform service fee.

Example:

* Service price: KES 500
* Platform service fee: KES 70
* Client invoice amount: KES 570
* Merchant platform fee responsibility: KES 70

The client invoice includes the full platform fee. The merchant still remains responsible for the full platform fee.

---

## 9.6 Does my service fee tier reduce the platform fee I owe?

No. The service fee tier affects the merchant-client invoice amount only.

It does not reduce the platform fee owed by the merchant to Citrus Labs Limited.

---

## 9.7 Where can I view platform fee records?

The Merchant Administrator can view merchant-level platform fee records where the platform enables this visibility.

These records may show:

* Charges
* Billing cycle
* Due date
* Amount paid
* Outstanding balance
* Adjustments
* Platform fee status
* Related branch or period details
* Statement or ledger information

---

## 9.8 Why is platform fee visibility important?

Platform fee visibility helps merchants understand what has been charged, what has been paid, and what remains outstanding.

This reduces confusion and supports cleaner business planning.

# 10. Preferred Personnel Waiting

## 10.1 What is preferred personnel waiting?

Preferred personnel waiting allows a client to choose a specific service provider instead of taking the next available personnel.

For example, a client may prefer a specific barber, stylist, therapist, or beautician.

---

## 10.2 Is preferred personnel selection required?

No. It is optional.

A client may choose:

1. Next available personnel
2. A specific preferred personnel

---

## 10.3 Can preferred personnel selection have an extra fee?

Yes. Where enabled, selecting preferred personnel can include an extra fee.

That fee is configured at platform level by the Super Administrator.

---

## 10.4 What should the client see before confirming preferred personnel?

The client-facing workflow should clearly show:

* The selected personnel
* The extra fee
* The estimated wait time
* The service being requested
* The final invoice impact

The client should understand the fee before confirmation.

---

## 10.5 How should the preferred personnel fee appear on an invoice?

The preferred personnel fee should appear as a separate invoice line item.

This makes the invoice easier to understand and helps reduce disputes.

---

## 10.6 What happens if the preferred personnel becomes unavailable?

The branch should be able to handle the situation clearly.

Possible outcomes may include:

* The client waits longer.
* The client is reassigned to another eligible personnel.
* The client cancels.
* The preferred personnel fee is reversed where appropriate.
* The invoice is adjusted where appropriate.

Every important change should be recorded.

---

Finance validation protects the business from mistakes and disputes.

It helps confirm:

* The amount paid
* The payment method
* The payment reference
* The related invoice
* Whether the payment is complete, partial, rejected, disputed, or needs correction

---

## 13.7 Can a receipt be generated before payment validation?

No. Receipts should only be generated after payment validation rules are satisfied.

This helps prevent premature receipts, duplicate receipts, and incorrect financial records.

---

## 13.8 What payment methods can be recorded?

Servana may support:

* Cash
* M-Pesa
* Bank transfer
* Card terminal
* Voucher
* Split payment

---

## 13.9 What is split payment?

Split payment means one invoice is paid using more than one payment method.

Example:

Each payment leg should have its own method, amount, reference where needed, and validation status.

---

## 13.10 What is partial payment?

Partial payment means the client has paid only part of the invoice amount.

Servana should show the remaining balance until the full validated amount matches the invoice amount.

# 14. Cash-Up and Reconciliation

## 14.1 What is branch cash-up?

Branch cash-up is the process of reviewing the day’s recorded payments and comparing them with expected totals.

It helps the business confirm whether branch payments, invoices, receipts, and payment records match.

---

## 14.2 Why is cash-up important?

Cash-up is important because Servana records offline payments.

Offline payments can create confusion when records are incomplete, references are missing, or cash is not counted properly.

A clear cash-up workflow helps reduce:

* Missing cash
* Payment disputes
* Unvalidated payments
* Wrong receipts
* Weak daily accountability
* Confusing reports

---

A cash-up review should include:

* Daily invoices
* Recorded payments
* Validated payments
* Pending validations
* Rejected payments
* Cash totals
* M-Pesa totals
* Bank transfer totals
* Card terminal totals
* Voucher totals
* Split payment totals
* Discrepancy notes
* Finance review status

---

## 14.4 Does the Merchant Administrator receive cash-up reports?

Yes. The Merchant Administrator should receive the daily branch cash-up and reconciliation report by email in PDF format.

---

## 14.5 Does the Merchant Administrator receive day-close reports?

Yes. The Merchant Administrator should receive the daily branch day-close report by email in PDF format.

---

## 14.6 What is day closing?

Day closing is the branch workflow used to confirm that daily activity has been reviewed.

It may include:

* Reviewing invoices
* Reviewing payment records
* Reviewing pending validations
* Reviewing receipts issued
* Reviewing offline totals
* Submitting the day-close record

---

## 14.7 Why should closed days be protected?

Closed days should not change silently.

If records change after closing, reports, commissions, receipts, and cash-up totals may become unreliable.

Where changes are needed, the platform should require a clear reason and activity record.

# 19. Security Basics

## 19.1 What security practices should my business follow?

Your business should:

* Use separate accounts for each user.
* Avoid shared email inboxes for individual users.
* Remove access when staff leave.
* Review roles regularly.
* Restrict finance and export permissions.
* Keep devices secure.
* Keep email accounts secure.
* Report suspicious activity quickly.
* Review audit logs.
* Use strong internal approval processes for sensitive actions.

---

## 19.2 Why should users not share accounts?

Shared accounts make activity records unclear.

If multiple people use one account, it becomes difficult to know who performed an action.

Every user should use their own account.

---

## 19.3 What should I do if a user’s email is compromised?

Act immediately.

You should:

1. Suspend the user’s Servana access.
2. Ask the user to secure their email account.
3. Review recent login activity.
4. Check activity logs for suspicious actions.
5. Invalidate active sessions where available.
6. Review role changes, exports, payments, invoices, and receipts.
7. Contact support through the authorized support workflow if needed.

---

## 19.4 What should I do if a staff member is suspended or deactivated?

When a staff member is suspended or deactivated:

* Their login should be blocked.
* Active sessions should be ended.
* Unused Magic Links should stop working.
* Live queues, appointments, or sessions should be reviewed.
* Historical records should remain visible for accountability.

---

## 19.5 Why is branch scope important for security?

Branch scope prevents users from seeing or changing records outside their assigned branch.

This protects client records, payment records, staff information, reports, and daily operations.

---
