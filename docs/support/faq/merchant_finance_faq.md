# 1. About Servana

## 1.1 What is Servana?

Servana is a service-operations SaaS platform for African service-based SMEs.

It helps merchants manage clients, staff, services, queues, appointments, invoices, commissions, platform fees, offline payment records, receipts, reports, and audit-ready operational records from one secure web dashboard.

Servana is not only a booking app, POS system, or invoicing tool. It is a daily operating system for service businesses.

---

## 1.2 Who is Servana built for?

Servana is built for service-based SMEs such as:

* Barbershops
* Salons
* Spas
* Massage parlours
* Grooming studios
* Beauty parlours
* Similar walk-in or appointment-based service businesses

It is especially useful for businesses that handle frequent client visits, staff commissions, branch activity, offline payments, and daily reconciliation.

---

## 1.3 What does Servana help merchants manage?

Servana helps merchants manage:

* Merchant onboarding
* Branches
* Staff access
* Client records
* Walk-ins
* Appointments
* Queues
* Service delivery
* Invoices
* Offline payment recording
* Payment validation
* Receipts
* Cash-up and reconciliation
* Staff commission visibility
* Platform fee visibility
* Reports
* Audit records

---

## 1.4 Does Servana process client payments?

No. Servana records payments that happen offline or outside the platform.

Payments may happen through:

* Cash
* M-Pesa
* Bank transfer
* Card terminal
* Voucher
* Split payment
* Other merchant-defined offline payment methods

Servana helps the business record, validate, reconcile, and review those payment details.

---

## 1.5 What is the difference between payment recording and payment validation?

Payment recording means a user enters the payment details into Servana.

Payment validation means Finance reviews the payment record and confirms whether it is acceptable.

For example, Front Office may record that a client paid by M-Pesa. Finance then checks the amount, invoice, reference, and status before the payment is treated as validated.

---

## 1.6 Why does Servana separate payment recording from validation?

Separating recording from validation gives the business stronger control.

It helps reduce:

* Incorrect payment records
* Missing payment references
* Duplicate payments
* Premature receipts
* Wrong invoice statuses
* Cash-up discrepancies
* Commission disputes
* Weak financial reports

This separation helps the branch run with more trust and accountability.

# 2. Merchant Finance Account Basics

## 2.1 What is the Merchant Finance account?

The Merchant Finance account is the role used to manage finance-related workflows for an assigned merchant branch.

It helps Finance users review invoices, validate offline payments, approve or reject payment records, generate receipts after validation, review cash-up records, manage disputes, review refunds recorded outside Servana, and monitor finance activity.

---

## 2.2 Who should use the Merchant Finance account?

The Merchant Finance account should be used by a trusted finance or accounting user.

Typical users may include:

* Branch finance officer
* Cash-up reviewer
* Finance administrator
* Accounts assistant
* Reconciliation officer
* Business owner acting in a finance role
* Senior branch reviewer
* Authorized finance operations user

This role should be assigned carefully because it can affect payment status, receipts, reports, and financial visibility.

---

## 2.3 What is the main purpose of the Merchant Finance account?

The main purpose is to keep financial records clear.

The Merchant Finance account helps answer practical questions such as:

* Which payments are pending validation?
* Which invoices are unpaid?
* Which payments were rejected?
* Which receipts are ready?
* Which payment references are missing?
* Which payments may be duplicated?
* Which cash-up records need review?
* Which branch totals do not match?
* Which refunds were recorded outside Servana?
* Which commission records depend on validated payments?
* Which finance exports were generated?
* Which platform fees are visible to this merchant or branch?

---

## 2.4 Is the Merchant Finance account a branch-level role?

Yes. Merchant Finance users are assigned within a merchant and branch scope.

A Finance user should not automatically access other branches. Where access is needed, it should be intentionally assigned through the correct merchant-side workflow.

---

## 2.5 Can Merchant Finance users access another merchant’s data?

No. Each merchant operates in its own tenant.

A Finance user should only access finance records for the merchant and branch they are assigned to.

---

## 2.6 Can one Finance user manage more than one branch?

This depends on merchant setup and permissions.

Where multi-branch finance access is allowed, it should be assigned carefully and clearly. The user should always know which branch they are reviewing.

---

## 2.7 How is the Merchant Finance account different from the Merchant Administrator account?

The Merchant Administrator manages the merchant account at a high level.

The Merchant Finance account focuses on finance workflows such as payment validation, receipts, cash-up, disputes, refunds, finance reports, and finance audit activity.

---

## 2.8 How is the Merchant Finance account different from the Front Office account?

Front Office handles client-facing activity, such as creating walk-ins, appointments, invoices, and recording payment details.

Finance reviews payment records, validates payments, handles financial checks, confirms receipts, reviews cash-up, and helps keep financial reports reliable.

---

## 2.9 How is the Merchant Finance account different from the Merchant Audit account?

Merchant Finance performs finance actions.

Merchant Audit reviews activity after it happens.

For example, Finance may validate a payment. Audit may later review who validated the payment, when it happened, and whether the action looks unusual.

# 3. Access and Login

## 3.1 How does a Merchant Finance user log in?

Merchant Finance users log in through a Magic Link sent to their email address.

The user requests access, receives a login link, and clicks the link to enter Servana.

---

## 3.2 Can a Finance user log in just because their email exists?

No. Servana checks more than the email address.

Before access is allowed, the platform checks that:

* The email exists.
* The user belongs to the correct merchant tenant.
* The user account is active.
* The Finance role is active.
* The user has not been suspended.
* The user is assigned to the correct branch where branch access applies.
* The Magic Link is valid, unused, and not expired.

This protects financial records from accidental or unauthorized access.

---

## 3.3 What should I do if my Magic Link does not work?

Request a new Magic Link first.

A Magic Link may fail because:

* It has expired.
* It was already used.
* It was opened from the wrong browser or device.
* You are checking the wrong email inbox.
* Your account is inactive.
* Your role is inactive.
* Your account has been suspended.
* Your branch assignment is missing or inactive.
* Your browser or email client blocked the login flow.

If the issue continues, contact support through the approved Help & Support workflow.

---

## 3.4 Can I share my Magic Link with another staff member?

No. Magic Links should not be shared.

Each user should access Servana through their own account. Sharing access makes financial records less reliable because the business cannot clearly tell who performed each action.

---

## 3.5 What should happen when a Finance user leaves the business?

The appropriate administrator should suspend, deactivate, or remove the user’s access promptly.

Past activity connected to that user should remain visible so the merchant can still understand payment validations, receipt actions, cash-up reviews, disputes, exports, and other finance activity.

* The amount is correct.

# 8. Payment Validation

## 8.1 What is payment validation?

Payment validation is the Finance review step that confirms whether a recorded payment should be accepted.

It is one of the most important workflows in the Merchant Finance account.

---

## 8.2 What payment validation statuses can exist?

Useful payment validation statuses include:

* Pending validation
* Validated
* Rejected
* Partially validated
* Disputed
* Correction requested
* Voided
* Refunded externally

The exact statuses may depend on the enabled workflow.

---

## 8.3 What does Pending Validation mean?

Pending validation means the payment has been recorded but Finance has not yet accepted or rejected it.

The payment is visible, but it should not be treated as fully confirmed.

---

## 8.4 What does Validated mean?

Validated means Finance has reviewed and accepted the payment record.

A validated payment can support receipt generation, confirmed payment status, reconciliation, and reporting.

---

## 8.5 What does Rejected mean?

Rejected means Finance reviewed the payment and did not accept it.

A payment may be rejected because:

* The amount is incorrect.
* The payment method is wrong.
* The payment reference is missing.
* The reference does not match available evidence.
* The payment appears duplicated.
* The wrong invoice was selected.
* The wrong branch was selected.
* The payment details are unclear.

---

## 8.6 What does Correction Requested mean?

Correction Requested means Finance needs the payment recorder or relevant branch user to update or clarify the record.

This is useful when the payment may be valid but some information is missing or incorrect.

---

## 8.7 What does Disputed mean?

Disputed means the payment needs further review.

A dispute may involve the client, branch, Finance, Merchant Administrator, or another internal reviewer depending on the issue.

---

## 8.8 What does Partially Validated mean?

Partially Validated means part of the payment is accepted, but the full invoice amount is not yet confirmed.

This can happen with partial payments or split payments.

---

## 8.9 Can Finance validate a payment without a reference?

Finance should be careful with missing references.

Some methods require strong references, such as M-Pesa transaction codes, bank references, card terminal references, or voucher codes. Cash may need a collector, amount, branch day, or cash-up reference.

If a reference is missing, Finance may reject the payment, request correction, or mark it for review depending on the payment method and branch workflow.

---

## 8.10 Should Finance add validation notes?

Yes. Validation notes are useful when a payment needs explanation.

Good notes are short, factual, and clear.

Examples:

* “M-Pesa code confirmed against daily M-Pesa summary.”
* “Cash amount matches branch cash-up count.”
* “Reference missing; correction requested.”
* “Duplicate reference detected; payment rejected pending review.”
* “Partial payment accepted; balance remains outstanding.”

---

# 9. Payment References

## 9.1 Why are payment references important?

Payment references help connect recorded payments to real payment evidence.

They support payment validation, cash-up, reconciliation, dispute handling, receipt accuracy, and audit review.

---

## 9.2 What reference is needed for cash payments?

Cash payments should include:

* Amount
* Collector or recording user
* Branch
* Date and time
* Cash-up or day-close reference where available
* Note where needed

Cash does not always have an external transaction code, so the internal branch record should be clear.

---

## 9.3 What reference is needed for M-Pesa payments?

M-Pesa payments should include the M-Pesa transaction code or reference.

Finance should check that the reference is clear, correctly entered, and not duplicated.

---

## 9.4 What reference is needed for bank transfers?

Bank transfers should include a bank reference, deposit slip reference, transaction reference, or other bank-provided identifier.

---

## 9.5 What reference is needed for card terminal payments?

Card terminal payments should include the terminal reference or transaction slip reference.

---

## 9.6 What reference is needed for voucher payments?

Voucher payments should include the voucher code and approval status where applicable.

---

## 9.7 What reference is needed for split payments?

Each split payment part should have its own method, amount, reference where needed, and validation status.

For example:

* Cash: KES 500, recorded by Front Office
* M-Pesa: KES 1,000, transaction code entered
* Voucher: KES 300, voucher code entered

Each part should be reviewable on its own.

---

## 9.8 What happens when a payment reference is duplicated?

A duplicate reference should be reviewed carefully.

The platform may block the duplicate, warn the user, or require an override with a reason, depending on configuration.

Finance should not ignore duplicate references because they can lead to double-counted payments or incorrect receipts.

---

# 10. Partial and Split Payments

## 10.1 What is a partial payment?

A partial payment means the client has paid only part of the invoice amount.

The invoice should continue to show the remaining balance until the full validated amount matches the invoice total.

---

## 10.2 What is a split payment?

A split payment means one invoice is paid using more than one method.

Example:

* KES 1,000 cash
* KES 2,000 M-Pesa

Each part should be recorded and validated properly.

---

## 10.3 Can one invoice have several payment records?

Yes. One invoice can have multiple payment records.

This is useful for partial payments, split payments, deposits, balances, and mixed payment methods.

---

## 10.4 Should each payment part be validated separately?

Yes. Each payment part should have its own validation status.

For example, the cash part may be validated, while the M-Pesa part remains pending because the transaction code is missing.

---

## 10.5 When should an invoice become paid?

An invoice should become paid only when the validated total equals the invoice total.

Recorded but unvalidated amounts should not automatically make an invoice paid.

---

## 10.6 How should receipts work for partial payments?

Receipts should show only validated payment amounts.

If the invoice is partially paid, the receipt should reflect the validated amount and the remaining balance where the workflow supports it.

---

# 11. Receipts

## 11.1 What is a receipt in Servana?

A receipt is the record issued after payment validation confirms that payment has been accepted.

It connects the payment, invoice, branch, client, amount, method, and receipt number.

---

## 11.2 When should a receipt be generated?

A receipt should be generated only after payment validation rules are satisfied.

This protects the business from issuing receipts for unconfirmed, rejected, duplicated, or incorrect payments.

---

## 11.3 Can Finance generate receipts?

Yes, where the Finance role has receipt permission.

Receipt generation should follow the configured validation rules.

---

## 11.4 Can a receipt be generated before validation?

No. A receipt should not be generated before payment validation.

Premature receipts can create client disputes, wrong reports, and incorrect financial records.

---

## 11.5 What should a receipt include?

A receipt should include:

* Receipt number
* Invoice number
* Branch
* Client reference
* Payment method
* Validated payment amount
* Receipt date
* Generated by
* Related payment record
* Reissue status where applicable

---

## 11.6 How should receipt numbers work?

Receipt numbers should be unique.

A branch prefix may be used for readability.

Examples:

* KIL-RCT-000124
* WES-RCT-000245

Duplicate receipt numbers should be prevented.

---

## 11.7 Can receipts be reissued?

Yes, where the workflow allows it.

A reissued receipt should reference the original receipt. This keeps the receipt history clear.

---

## 11.8 Can receipts be deleted?

Receipts should not be casually deleted.

Where a receipt needs correction, the platform should preserve the original record and use a reversal, cancellation, or reissue workflow depending on configuration.

---

## 11.9 Why should receipt downloads be logged?

Receipt downloads may matter during client queries, payment disputes, or internal reviews.

Logging downloads helps the business understand who accessed or downloaded a receipt and when.

---

# 12. Outstanding Balances

## 12.1 What is an outstanding balance?

An outstanding balance is the amount still unpaid or not yet validated on an invoice or finance record.

---

## 12.2 Why does Finance need to review outstanding balances?

Outstanding balances help Finance see what is still due.

They also help the branch understand which clients, invoices, or payment records need follow-up.

---

## 12.3 What can cause an outstanding balance?

An outstanding balance may happen because:

* The client made a partial payment.
* A payment is pending validation.
* A payment was rejected.
* A payment reference is missing.
* A split payment is incomplete.
* An invoice was adjusted.
* A refund was recorded externally.
* A payment was entered against the wrong invoice.

---

## 12.4 Can an invoice look paid before Finance validation?

It should not be treated as fully paid until validation rules are satisfied.

A recorded payment may show that a payment was entered, but Finance validation confirms whether that payment is accepted.

---

# 13. Cash-Up and Reconciliation

## 13.1 What is branch cash-up?

Branch cash-up is the process of reviewing the day’s recorded payments and comparing them against expected totals.

Because Servana records offline payments, cash-up is a key finance workflow.

---

## 13.2 What is reconciliation?

Reconciliation means checking whether invoices, payment records, receipts, and actual offline payment totals match.

It helps the business find missing payments, wrong amounts, duplicate references, rejected payments, unissued receipts, and cash differences.

---

## 13.3 What should Finance review during cash-up?

Finance should review:

* Daily invoices
* Recorded payments
* Validated payments
* Pending validations
* Rejected payments
* Cash total
* M-Pesa total
* Bank transfer total
* Card terminal total
* Voucher total
* Split payment total
* Expected revenue
* Recorded total
* Discrepancy amount
* Notes from the branch
* Receipts issued
* Receipts pending

---

## 13.4 Can Finance approve or reject branch cash-up?

Yes, where the workflow allows it.

Finance may approve, reject, or flag cash-up records depending on the review outcome.

---

## 13.5 What is a cash-up discrepancy?

A discrepancy is a mismatch between expected totals and actual or recorded totals.

Examples include:

* Cash counted does not match recorded cash payments.
* M-Pesa total does not match transaction references.
* A payment is recorded twice.
* An invoice is unpaid but service is marked complete.
* A receipt is missing for a validated payment.
* A payment remains pending validation.
* A payment was rejected but included in the total.

---

## 13.6 What should Finance do when there is a discrepancy?

Finance should record a clear note and follow the merchant’s review process.

The note should explain:

* What does not match
* Which branch is affected
* Which invoice or payment records are involved
* What action is needed
* Who should follow up

---

## 13.7 Why is cash-up important for cash-heavy businesses?

Cash-heavy businesses need strong daily accountability.

Cash can be misplaced, miscounted, entered late, or recorded incorrectly. Cash-up helps the branch and merchant confirm that the day’s financial records are complete and understandable.

---

## 13.8 Should the Merchant Administrator receive cash-up reports?

Yes. The Merchant Administrator should receive the daily branch cash-up and reconciliation report by email in PDF format.

---

# 14. Financial Periods

## 14.1 What is a financial period?

A financial period is a defined time range used for finance review.

Examples include:

* A branch day
* A week
* A month
* A custom reporting period

---

## 14.2 What does it mean to lock a financial period?

Locking a financial period means the records for that period should not change silently.

This protects reports, receipts, commissions, cash-up summaries, and historical financial visibility.

---

## 14.3 Can Finance lock periods?

This depends on merchant configuration.

Some merchants may allow senior Finance users to lock periods. Others may reserve period locking for the Merchant Administrator.

---

## 14.4 What should happen after a period is locked?

After a period is locked:

* Reports should stay stable.
* Edits should be restricted.
* Reopening should require a reason.
* Changes should be recorded.
* Users should understand that the period has been reviewed.

---

## 14.5 Can a locked period be reopened?

Yes, where the workflow allows it.

Reopening should require a clear reason and should be recorded in the audit logs.

---

## 14.6 Why should locked periods not change silently?

Silent changes can affect revenue reports, commissions, receipts, cash-up, and management decisions.

A clear reopening workflow protects trust in financial records.

---

# 15. Payment Disputes

## 15.1 What is a payment dispute?

A payment dispute is a finance issue that needs review before it can be treated as resolved.

It may involve a client, invoice, payment record, reference, receipt, branch user, or finance reviewer.

---

## 15.2 When should Finance open a dispute?

Finance may open a dispute when:

* Payment evidence is unclear.
* The amount does not match the invoice.
* The reference appears duplicated.
* A client says they paid but the record is missing.
* A branch user recorded the wrong method.
* A receipt was issued incorrectly.
* A payment was rejected but later needs review.
* A refund needs review.
* A split payment is incomplete or unclear.

---

## 15.3 What dispute statuses can exist?

Useful dispute statuses include:

* Open
* Under review
* Evidence requested
* Resolved
* Rejected
* Escalated
* Closed

---

## 15.4 What information should a dispute record include?

A dispute record should include:

* Dispute type
* Invoice number
* Payment record
* Raised by
* Assigned to
* Amount in dispute
* Reason
* Evidence attachment where available
* Resolution note
* Resolved by
* Resolved date and time

---

## 15.5 Can Finance resolve disputes?

Yes, where the workflow allows it.

Finance may review the evidence, update the dispute status, and record a resolution note.

---

## 15.6 What makes a good dispute note?

A good dispute note is clear, factual, and specific.

Example:

“Client states M-Pesa payment was made at 3:14 PM. Transaction code entered by Front Office does not match the client screenshot. Evidence requested from branch.”

---

## 15.7 Should disputes be handled outside Servana?

Servana should be used to keep the dispute record clear.

A team may discuss the issue outside Servana, but the important outcome, evidence reference, and resolution should be recorded in Servana where the workflow allows it.

---

---

## 16.2 What is an external refund record?

An external refund record is a Servana record showing that a refund happened outside the platform.

## 18.4 Does the merchant service fee tier reduce the merchant’s platform fee responsibility?

No. The service fee tier affects merchant-client invoice pricing only.

The merchant remains responsible for the full platform fee amount shown in the platform fee records.

---

## 18.5 What are the service fee tiers?

Servana may support:

* Customer Centric
* Split Tier
* Business Centric

These tiers affect how the merchant-client invoice displays the platform service fee impact.

---

## 18.6 What does Customer Centric mean?

Customer Centric means the client pays only the merchant’s service price.

Example:

* Service price: KES 500
* Platform service fee: KES 70
* Client invoice amount: KES 500
* Merchant platform fee responsibility: KES 70

The merchant absorbs the platform fee.

---

## 18.7 What does Split Tier mean?

Split Tier means the client invoice includes part of the platform fee.

Example:

* Service price: KES 500
* Platform service fee: KES 70
* Client invoice amount: KES 535
* Merchant platform fee responsibility: KES 70

The client invoice includes 50% of the platform fee. The merchant still remains responsible for the full platform fee.

---

## 18.8 What does Business Centric mean?

Business Centric means the client invoice includes the full platform fee.

Example:

* Service price: KES 500
* Platform service fee: KES 70
* Client invoice amount: KES 570
* Merchant platform fee responsibility: KES 70

The client invoice includes the full platform fee. The merchant still remains responsible for the full platform fee.

---

## 18.9 Can Finance raise a platform fee question?

Yes, where the workflow allows it.

Finance or the Merchant Administrator may raise a platform fee question when a charge, payment, adjustment, due date, or statement needs review.

# 23. Common Finance Scenarios

## 23.1 A payment was recorded with the wrong amount. What should I do?

Review the invoice, payment method, and supporting reference.

Depending on the workflow, you may:

* Reject the payment.
* Request correction.
* Edit the payment reference or amount if you have permission.
* Mark the payment as disputed.
* Add a validation note.
* Ask the branch user to correct the record.

Avoid validating a payment when the amount is unclear.

---

## 23.2 A payment reference is missing. What should I do?

Check the payment method.

For M-Pesa, bank transfer, card terminal, and voucher payments, a reference is usually important. You may request correction, reject the payment, or mark it for review depending on the situation.

For cash, check the cash-up details, collector, amount, and branch-day record.

---

## 23.3 The same M-Pesa reference appears twice. What should I do?

Treat this as a high-priority review item.

Check whether:

* The same payment was entered twice.
* The reference was mistyped.
* The payment belongs to another invoice.
* The client paid twice.
* A user reused a reference incorrectly.

Do not accept both records unless the issue is clearly explained and the workflow allows it.

---

## 23.4 A receipt is ready but not generated. What should I do?

Check whether the payment is fully validated.

If the payment is validated and receipt permission is available, generate the receipt according to the workflow.

If the payment is only partially validated or pending validation, do not generate the full receipt yet.

---

## 23.5 A branch cash-up does not match. What should I do?

Review the mismatch and record a clear discrepancy note.

Check:

* Cash total
* M-Pesa references
* Bank transfers
* Card terminal references
* Voucher records
* Split payments
* Pending validations
* Rejected payments
* Receipts issued
* Unpaid invoices

Then approve, reject, or flag the cash-up according to the workflow.

---

## 23.6 A paid invoice needs to be voided. What should I do?

Handle it carefully.

Paid invoice voiding can affect receipts, payments, commissions, reports, and client records. Follow the configured approval workflow and record a clear reason.

---

## 23.7 A client says they paid, but the payment is not visible. What should I do?

Ask for the payment evidence through the branch’s internal process.

Check:

* Invoice number
* Client details
* Payment method
* Amount
* Date and time
* M-Pesa code, bank reference, card slip, or voucher code
* Branch
* User who may have recorded the payment

If the record is missing, the correct operational user may need to enter it before Finance can validate it.

---

## 23.8 A payment was rejected by mistake. What should I do?

Follow the correction or dispute workflow.

Depending on permissions, the payment may need to be reopened, corrected, or re-entered. Add a clear note explaining what happened.

---

# 25. Security Basics

## 25.1 What should Finance users do to keep access secure?

Finance users should:

* Use their own email account.
* Never share Magic Links.
* Keep email accounts secure.
* Lock their devices.
* Avoid shared browsers.
* Log out on shared devices.
* Avoid unnecessary downloads.
* Review payment records carefully.
* Report suspicious activity quickly.
* Keep financial information confidential within the business.

---

## 25.2 Why is account sharing a problem?

Account sharing makes finance records unclear.

If several people use one Finance account, the business cannot confidently tell who validated a payment, rejected a payment, generated a receipt, approved cash-up, exported a report, or reopened a period.

Each user should use their own account.

---

## 25.3 What should Finance do when suspicious activity appears?

Finance should:

1. Review the affected record.
2. Check the branch, user, date, and module.
3. Look for related invoice, payment, receipt, cash-up, or export activity.
4. Flag the issue internally.
5. Notify the Merchant Administrator or assigned manager.
6. Use the Help & Support workflow if platform support is needed.

---

## 25.4 What should Finance check after suspected account compromise?

Check:

* Recent login activity
* Payment validations
* Rejected payments
* Payment reference edits
* Receipts generated
* Receipt reissues
* Refund records
* Invoice voids
* Cash-up reviews
* Report exports
* Period locks or reopenings
* Role or permission changes
