# 1. About Servana

## 1.1 What is Servana?

Servana is a service-operations SaaS platform for African service-based SMEs.

It helps merchants manage clients, staff, services, queues, appointments, invoices, offline payment records, receipts, commissions, platform fees, permissions, reports, and audit-ready records from one secure dashboard.

Servana is not only a booking app, POS system, or invoicing tool. It is a daily operating system for service businesses.

---

## 1.2 Who is Servana built for?

Servana is built for service-based SMEs such as:

* Barbershops
* Salons
* Spas
* Massage parlours
* Grooming studios
* Beauty parlours
* Similar walk-in or appointment-based service businesses

It is especially useful where a branch handles many clients, service providers, queues, appointments, invoices, and offline payments during the day.

---

## 1.3 What does Servana help the Front Office team manage?

The Front Office team can use Servana to manage:

* Client lookup
* New client registration
* Walk-ins
* Appointments
* Client check-in
* Queue entries
* Service selection
* Next available personnel assignment
* Preferred personnel selection
* Service sessions
* Invoice generation
* Offline payment recording
* Awaiting Finance validation status
* Ready-for-receipt status
* Daily branch activity
* Speed-sensitive search
* Client-facing service flow

The role helps the front desk keep the branch moving.

---

## 1.4 Does Servana process client payments?

No. Servana records payments that happen offline or outside the platform.

A client may pay through cash, M-Pesa, bank transfer, card terminal, voucher, split payment, or another merchant-defined offline method.

The Front Office user records the payment details in Servana. Finance then validates the payment where validation is enabled.

---

## 1.5 Why is the Front Office account important?

Front Office is often where the client journey begins.

A clear Front Office workflow helps the branch know:

* Who arrived
* Who has an appointment
* Who is walking in
* Which service the client wants
* Which personnel can perform the service
* Whether the client chose next available or preferred personnel
* Where the client is in the queue
* Whether the service is complete
* Whether the invoice has been created
* Whether payment has been recorded
* Whether Finance still needs to validate the payment
* Whether the receipt is ready

Good Front Office work keeps the whole branch organized.

# 2. Merchant Front Office Account Basics

## 2.1 What is the Merchant Front Office account?

The Merchant Front Office account is the role used for client-facing branch operations.

It helps the branch team register clients, retrieve existing clients, create walk-ins, create appointments, check clients in, manage queue activity, select services, assign personnel, create service sessions, generate invoices, record offline payments, and view payment-validation progress.

---

## 2.2 Who should use the Merchant Front Office account?

This account should be used by branch users who handle client-facing activity.

Typical users may include:

* Receptionist
* Front desk operator
* Client service desk user
* Branch service coordinator
* Appointment desk user
* Queue coordinator
* Walk-in desk user
* Branch operations assistant

The user should be comfortable handling clients, checking details carefully, and keeping records accurate during busy branch hours.

---

## 2.3 Is the Front Office account branch-specific?

Yes. The Front Office account is assigned to a specific merchant and branch.

A Front Office user should only see and manage records for the branch they are assigned to, unless additional access is intentionally configured.

---

## 2.4 Can a Front Office user manage another branch?

Not by default.

A Front Office user should not automatically access another branch. If the business wants a user to work across more than one branch, the correct administrator or HR workflow should assign that access clearly.

---

## 2.5 Can a Front Office user manage another merchant’s data?

No.

Each merchant operates separately in Servana. A Front Office user should only access records for the merchant and branch they belong to.

---

## 2.6 What is the main purpose of the Front Office account?

The main purpose is to keep the client-service flow moving smoothly.

The account helps answer questions like:

* Has the client visited before?
* Does the client have an appointment?
* Is this a walk-in?
* Which service does the client want?
* Which personnel can perform the service?
* Does the client want next available personnel or preferred personnel?
* Where is the client in the queue?
* Has the service started?
* Has the service been completed?
* Has the invoice been generated?
* Has payment been recorded?
* Is the payment awaiting Finance validation?
* Is the receipt ready?

# 3. Access and Login

## 3.1 How does a Front Office user log in?

Front Office users log in through a Magic Link sent to their email address.

The user requests access, receives a login link, and clicks the link to enter Servana.

---

## 3.2 Can a Front Office user log in just because their email exists?

No. Servana checks more than the email address.

Before access is allowed, the platform checks that:

* The email exists.
* The user belongs to the correct merchant tenant.
* The user account is active.
* The Front Office role is active.
* The user has not been suspended.
* The user is assigned to the correct branch.
* The Magic Link is valid, unused, and not expired.

This protects branch records from accidental or unauthorized access.

---

## 3.3 What should I do if my Magic Link does not work?

Request a new Magic Link first.

A Magic Link may fail because:

* It expired.
* It was already used.
* It was opened from the wrong browser or device.
* You are checking the wrong email inbox.
* Your account is inactive.
* Your role is inactive.
* Your account has been suspended.
* Your branch assignment is missing or inactive.
* Your browser or email client blocked the login flow.

If the issue continues, contact support through the approved Help & Support workflow.

---

## 3.4 Can I share my Magic Link with another staff member?

No. Magic Links should not be shared.

Each user should access Servana through their own account. Sharing access makes activity records unclear and weakens branch accountability.

---

## 3.5 What should happen when a Front Office user leaves the business?

The appropriate administrator or HR user should suspend, deactivate, or remove the user’s access.

Past activity connected to that user should remain visible so the branch can still understand client registration, appointment, queue, invoice, and payment-record activity.

# 4. Front Office Dashboard

## 4.1 What is the Front Office dashboard?

The Front Office dashboard is the main working screen for client-facing branch operations.

It helps the user see what is happening now, what needs action next, and which clients are waiting, assigned, in service, completed, or awaiting payment steps.

---

## 4.2 What should the Front Office dashboard show?

The dashboard may show:

* Next client
* Waiting clients
* Clients in service
* Completed clients today
* Current appointments
* Current walk-ins
* Preferred personnel requests
* Payment records pending Finance validation
* Ready-for-receipt items
* Pending commission-related service activity where visible
* Queue delays
* No-shows
* Cancelled sessions

The dashboard should be fast, clear, and easy to use during busy hours.

---

## 4.3 Why does Front Office need a fast dashboard?

Front Office users often work while clients are waiting.

They need to search quickly, check in clients, assign services, update queues, and move clients through the branch without confusion.

A slow or scattered workflow can create long queues, duplicate records, missed appointments, and wrong invoices.

---

## 4.4 What does “Next Client” mean?

“Next Client” shows the next client who needs attention.

This may be the next person waiting, the next appointment to check in, the next client ready for assignment, or the next client ready for invoicing depending on branch workflow.

---

## 4.5 What does “Waiting” mean?

“Waiting” means the client is in the branch flow but has not yet started service.

A waiting client may be:

* A walk-in waiting for assignment
* An appointment that has checked in
* A client waiting for preferred personnel
* A client waiting because personnel is busy
* A client waiting because the queue is full or delayed

---

## 4.6 What does “In Service” mean?

“In Service” means the client has started the selected service with assigned personnel.

This status helps the branch know who is currently being served.

---

## 4.7 What does “Completed Today” mean?

“Completed Today” shows the clients whose service sessions have been completed during the current branch day.

These records may still need invoicing, payment recording, Finance validation, or receipt follow-up depending on the workflow.

---

# 5. Client Records

## 5.1 What is a client record?

A client record stores useful information about a client who receives services from the branch.

It may include:

* Client name
* Phone number
* Email, where available
* Visit history
* Service history
* Appointment history
* Queue history
* Assigned personnel history
* Preferred personnel history
* Invoice history
* Receipt history
* Notes
* Communication preferences, where configured

---

## 5.2 Can Front Office register new clients?

Yes. Front Office users can register new clients within their assigned branch workflow.

The user should enter accurate details because client records connect to appointments, services, invoices, receipts, and future visits.

---

## 5.3 Can Front Office retrieve existing clients?

Yes. Front Office users can search for and retrieve existing clients.

This helps prevent duplicate records and makes repeat visits smoother.

---

## 5.4 Why should Front Office search before creating a new client?

Searching first helps avoid duplicate client records.

Duplicate records can split the client’s history, create confusion during invoicing, and make reporting less reliable.

---

## 5.5 What details should Front Office check before creating a new client?

Before creating a new client, check:

* Phone number
* Client name
* Email, where available
* Existing appointment reference
* Previous visit records
* Branch record match

The phone number is often the fastest search key during busy branch operations.

---

## 5.6 Can a client exist in more than one branch?

Yes. A client may appear in more than one branch.

However, duplicate records within the same branch should be prevented or handled through a controlled workflow.

---

## 5.7 What should I do if I find duplicate client records?

Do not create another record.

Follow the branch’s duplicate-client process. If the platform supports merge or review workflows, use the approved process. If not, notify the correct branch manager, HR, Audit, or support contact depending on the issue.

---

# 6. Speed-Sensitive Search

## 6.1 What is speed-sensitive search?

Speed-sensitive search helps Front Office users find records quickly during active client service.

It is designed for busy branch environments where the user needs the right record immediately.

---

## 6.2 What can Front Office search by?

Front Office search should support:

* Phone number
* Client name
* Appointment reference
* Invoice number
* Receipt number
* Queue position

These search options help the user move quickly from client arrival to the right workflow.

---

## 6.3 Why is phone number search important?

Phone number search is often the fastest way to find a returning client.

It reduces spelling issues and helps avoid duplicate client registration.

---

## 6.4 Why is appointment reference search important?

Appointment reference search helps the user quickly check in clients who booked ahead.

It is useful when several clients arrive at the same time.

---

## 6.5 Why is invoice or receipt search useful for Front Office?

Clients may return to ask about a payment, receipt, invoice, or service record.

Invoice and receipt search helps the Front Office user quickly locate the record and guide the client to the next step.

---

## 6.6 Why is queue position search useful?

Queue position search helps the branch find a waiting client quickly.

This is useful in fast-moving walk-in environments where clients may ask how long they still need to wait.

# 7. Walk-Ins

## 7.1 What is a walk-in?

A walk-in is a client who arrives at the branch without a scheduled appointment.

The Front Office user can create a walk-in session and move the client into the queue or service flow.

---

## 7.2 Can Front Office create walk-ins?

Yes. Front Office users can create walk-in sessions for the assigned branch.

---

## 7.3 What should happen when a walk-in is created?

Creating a walk-in should connect or create:

* Client record
* Selected service
* Branch
* Queue entry
* Assignment mode
* Optional preferred personnel selection
* Optional preferred personnel fee, where enabled

This keeps the walk-in connected to the full branch workflow.

---

## 7.4 Why should walk-in creation be handled carefully?

A walk-in can affect queue order, service assignment, invoice creation, payment records, staff performance, and client history.

A rushed or incomplete walk-in record can create confusion later.

---

## 7.5 Can a walk-in become a service session?

Yes. A walk-in can move through the queue and become a service session once assigned and started.

---

## 7.6 Can a walk-in be cancelled?

Yes. A walk-in can be cancelled where the workflow allows it.

A cancellation reason should be recorded so the branch understands why the client did not continue.

---

## 7.7 What are common walk-in cancellation reasons?

Common reasons include:

* Client left
* Client changed mind
* Client rescheduled
* Queue too long
* Preferred personnel unavailable
* Service unavailable
* Duplicate entry
* Branch closed
* Incorrect record created

---

Check-in confirms that the client has arrived and is ready to be served.

It helps the branch move an appointment or walk-in into the correct active workflow.

---

## 9.2 Why is check-in important?

Check-in prevents confusion.

It tells the branch:

* The client is physically present or ready.
* The appointment should now move into active handling.
* The client can be placed in the queue.

* The branch should start tracking the client’s service flow.

## 9.3 Can Front Office check in appointment clients?

Yes. Front Office can check in appointment clients where enabled.

---

## 9.4 Can Front Office check in walk-in clients?

The user should still confirm the client details, selected service, and assignment mode.

---

## 9.5 What should I check before confirming check-in?

Check:

* Client name
* Phone number

# 10. Queue Management

## 10.1 What is the branch queue?

The branch queue is the list of clients waiting for service.

It helps the branch track who is waiting, who has been assigned, who is in service, who completed service, who cancelled, and who did not show.

---

## 10.2 Can Front Office manage queue status?

Yes. Front Office users can manage queue status where enabled.

This may include checking in, assigning, starting service, moving clients through the queue, recording cancellations, and viewing readiness for invoice or payment steps.

---

## 10.3 What queue statuses should Front Office understand?

Useful queue statuses include:

* Waiting
* Assigned
* In service
* Completed
* Cancelled
* No-show

---

## 10.4 What does Waiting mean?

Waiting means the client is in the queue but service has not started.

---

## 10.5 What does Assigned mean?

Assigned means the client has been assigned to eligible personnel, but the service may not have started yet.

---

## 10.6 What does In Service mean?

In Service means the client is currently receiving the selected service.

---

## 10.7 What does Completed mean?

Completed means the service has been finished.

After completion, the client may still need invoicing, payment recording, Finance validation, or receipt follow-up.

---

## 10.8 What does Cancelled mean?

Cancelled means the client’s queue entry or service flow was stopped before completion.

A cancellation reason should be recorded.

---

## 10.9 What does No-show mean?

No-show means the client did not arrive or did not respond when expected.

This is especially useful for appointments.

---

## 10.10 What is an actionable queue?

An actionable queue is a queue where each item has a clear next action.

Examples of actions include:

* Check in
* Assign
* Start service
* Invoice
* Record payment
* Await Finance validation
* Ready for receipt

This helps the Front Office user know what to do next.

---

## 10.11 Can Front Office reorder the queue?

Queue reordering depends on branch permissions.

Where queue reordering is allowed, the reason should be clear and the action should be recorded.

---

## 10.12 Why should queue changes be recorded?

Queue changes affect client wait time, staff workload, invoice timing, and service flow.

Recording queue activity helps the branch explain what happened during the day.

---

# 11. Personnel Assignment

## 11.1 Can Front Office assign personnel to a client?

Yes. Front Office can assign personnel where the workflow allows it.

The selected personnel should be eligible to perform the selected service.

---

## 11.2 What does next available personnel mean?

Next available personnel means the client is assigned to the next eligible staff member who can perform the selected service.

This is useful when the client does not have a preferred service provider.

---

## 11.3 What does preferred personnel mean?

Preferred personnel means the client chooses a specific service provider.

This is common in barbershops, salons, spas, massage parlours, grooming studios, and similar service businesses where clients often prefer a specific barber, stylist, therapist, beautician, or specialist.

---

## 11.4 Can Front Office assign any personnel to any service?

No. Personnel should only be assigned to services they are eligible to perform.

Service eligibility is controlled by the HR workflow, not by Front Office guesswork.

---

## 11.5 Why is service eligibility important?

Service eligibility helps protect service quality and branch order.

It helps prevent a client from being assigned to someone who is not approved or prepared to perform the selected service.

---

## 11.6 What should I do if the right personnel is not shown?

Check:

* Whether the personnel is assigned to the branch
* Whether the personnel is active
* Whether the personnel is available
* Whether the personnel is eligible for the selected service
* Whether the branch is open
* Whether the service is active
* Whether the queue mode allows that assignment

If the issue continues, notify the Branch account user, HR user, or Authorized Support Contact.

---

# 12. Preferred Personnel Waiting

## 12.1 What is preferred personnel waiting?

Preferred personnel waiting allows a client to wait for a specific service provider instead of taking the next available personnel.

---

## 12.2 Is preferred personnel optional?

Yes. The client may choose:

* Next available personnel
* A specific preferred personnel

---

## 12.3 Can preferred personnel have an extra fee?

Yes. Where enabled, preferred personnel selection can include an extra fee.

The fee should be shown clearly before the client confirms.

---

## 12.4 What should Front Office show the client before confirming preferred personnel?

The client should understand:

* The selected service
* The selected preferred personnel
* The extra fee
* The estimated wait time
* The final invoice impact

This helps avoid confusion later.

---

## 12.5 How should the preferred personnel fee appear?

The preferred personnel fee should appear as a separate invoice line item.

This makes the invoice and receipt easier to understand.

---

## 12.6 What happens if preferred personnel becomes unavailable?

The branch may need to handle one of the following:

* Client waits longer
* Client is reassigned to another eligible personnel
* Client cancels
* Preferred fee is reversed where appropriate
* Invoice is adjusted where appropriate

The action should be recorded.

---

## 12.7 Can Front Office override preferred personnel?

Front Office may override preferred personnel only where permission allows it.

An override should require a clear reason and should be recorded in the activity logs.

---

## 12.8 What are good override reasons?

Good override reasons may include:

* Preferred personnel became unavailable
* Client agreed to another personnel
* Emergency branch change
* Duplicate queue entry corrected
* Service reassigned to eligible personnel
* Appointment conflict resolved

Avoid vague notes such as “changed” or “updated.”

---

# 13. Service Sessions

## 13.1 What is a service session?

A service session records the actual service flow for a client.

It connects:

* Client
* Branch
* Selected service
* Assigned personnel
* Queue or appointment record
* Start status
* Completion status
* Invoice flow
* Payment flow

---

## 13.2 Can Front Office create service sessions?

Yes. Front Office can create service sessions where enabled.

A service session may be created from a walk-in, appointment, or queue entry.

---

## 13.3 What statuses can a service session have?

Useful statuses include:

* Draft
* Waiting
* Assigned
* In progress
* Completed
* Cancelled
* Invoiced
* Paid

The exact status names may depend on the workflow.

---

## 13.4 What is the correct service-session flow?

A practical flow is:

Waiting → Assigned → In Service → Completed → Invoiced → Payment Recorded → Awaiting Finance Validation → Ready for Receipt

The platform should prevent invalid jumps where possible.

---

## 13.5 Why should duplicate service sessions be avoided?

Duplicate service sessions can cause:

* Duplicate invoices
* Wrong commission records
* Confused client history
* Wrong service reports
* Payment disputes
* Staff performance errors

Always check whether a session already exists before creating a new one.

---

## 13.6 Can Front Office cancel a service session?

Yes, where the workflow allows it.

A cancellation reason should be recorded.

---

## 13.7 What are common service-session cancellation reasons?

Common reasons include:

* Client left
* Client changed service
* Service unavailable
* Personnel unavailable
* Duplicate session
* Wrong client selected
* Wrong service selected
* Branch closure
* Client rescheduled

---

# 14. Invoices

## 14.1 Can Front Office generate invoices?

Yes. Front Office can generate invoices where the branch workflow allows it.

The invoice should be connected to the client, branch, service, assigned personnel, service price, preferred personnel fee where applicable, and final amount.

---

## 14.2 When should an invoice be generated?

An invoice is usually generated after the selected service is confirmed and the branch is ready to bill the client.

Depending on workflow, this may happen before or after service completion.

---

## 14.3 What should an invoice include?

An invoice should include:

* Invoice number
* Merchant
* Branch
* Client
* Service
* Assigned personnel
* Service price
* Discount where applied
* Preferred personnel fee where applied
* Final amount
* Payment status
* Created by
* Date and time

---

## 14.4 Can Front Office change invoice details?

Only where permission allows it.

Invoice changes should be handled carefully because they affect payment recording, Finance validation, receipts, reports, and commissions.

---

## 14.5 Can Front Office void invoices?

Invoice voiding depends on permissions.

Where voiding is allowed, a clear reason should be recorded. Paid invoices should be handled with extra care and may require Finance or administrator review.

---

## 14.6 Why should invoices be linked to service sessions?

Linking invoices to service sessions helps the branch confirm what was provided, who served the client, what was charged, and how the payment should be handled.

---

## 14.7 What happens if an invoice is created for the wrong client?

Do not ignore it.

Follow the branch correction process. Depending on the workflow, the invoice may need correction, voiding, or review by Finance or the Branch account user.

# 15. Offline Payment Recording

## 15.1 Can Front Office record payments?

Yes. Front Office can record offline payment details where enabled.

Recording a payment does not mean the payment has been fully validated.

---

## 15.2 What payment methods can Front Office record?

Front Office may record:

* Cash
* M-Pesa
* Bank transfer
* Card terminal
* Voucher
* Split payment
* Other merchant-defined offline payment methods

---

## 15.3 What payment details should be recorded?

A payment record should include:

* Invoice number
* Payment method
* Payment amount
* Payment reference where applicable
* Payment date and time
* Branch
* Recording user
* Notes where helpful

---

## 15.4 What is a payment reference?

A payment reference is the code or detail that helps confirm the payment.

Examples include:

* M-Pesa transaction code
* Bank transfer reference
* Card terminal reference
* Voucher code
* Cash collector or cash-up reference

---

## 15.5 Why should Front Office enter payment references carefully?

Payment references help Finance validate payments.

A missing, wrong, or duplicate reference can delay validation, receipt generation, and daily cash-up.

---

## 15.6 What is split payment?

Split payment means one invoice is paid using more than one payment method.

Example:

* KES 500 cash
* KES 1,000 M-Pesa

Each payment part should be recorded clearly.

---

## 15.7 What is partial payment?

Partial payment means the client has paid only part of the invoice amount.

The unpaid balance should remain visible until payment is completed and validated.

---

## 15.8 What should Front Office do after recording a payment?

After recording payment details, Front Office should check the payment status.

The payment may show as:

* Saved
* Unsaved
* Pending validation
* Awaiting Finance validation
* Ready for receipt after validation

The exact label may depend on the workflow.

---

# 16. Saved, Unsaved, and Pending Validation

## 16.1 What does Unsaved mean?

Unsaved means the payment details have not yet been fully saved in Servana.

Do not assume the payment record exists until the system confirms it was saved.

---

## 16.2 What does Saved mean?

Saved means the payment details have been recorded in Servana.

The payment may still need Finance validation.

---

## 16.3 What does Pending Validation mean?

Pending validation means Finance has not yet approved or rejected the payment record.

This is common when Front Office records payment details and Finance reviews them later.

---

## 16.4 What does Awaiting Finance Validation mean?

Awaiting Finance Validation means the payment record has been submitted for Finance review.

Front Office should not treat the payment as fully confirmed until Finance validation is complete.

---

## 16.5 What does Ready for Receipt mean?

Ready for Receipt means the payment has reached a valid state for receipt generation.

Depending on the workflow, Finance or another permitted role may generate the receipt.

---

## 16.6 Can Front Office issue receipts before Finance validation?

No. Front Office should not issue a receipt without a linked invoice and valid payment state.

This helps prevent premature receipts and payment disputes.

---

# 17. Receipts

## 17.1 Can Front Office view receipt status?

Yes. Front Office can view receipt readiness where enabled.

This helps the user answer client questions without performing Finance-only actions.

---

## 17.2 Can Front Office generate receipts?

This depends on merchant configuration.

Where Front Office is not permitted to generate receipts, the user should wait for Finance validation and direct the client according to the branch workflow.

---

## 17.3 When should a receipt be generated?

A receipt should be generated only after the invoice and payment record are in the correct state.

If Finance validation is enabled, receipt generation should normally wait until payment is validated.

---

## 17.4 Why should receipts not be issued too early?

Early receipts can create confusion if the payment is later rejected, corrected, disputed, or found to be duplicated.

A clean receipt process protects the client, the branch, and the merchant.

---

## 17.5 What should Front Office do if a client asks for a receipt?

Check the record status.

If the receipt is ready, follow the branch workflow. If the payment is pending validation, explain that the payment has been recorded and is awaiting Finance confirmation.

---

# 21. Common Front Office Scenarios

## 21.1 A returning client arrives. What should I do?

Search for the client first.

Use phone number, name, appointment reference, or another available search option. If the client already exists, use the existing record instead of creating a new one.

---

## 21.2 A new client arrives without an appointment. What should I do?

Create a walk-in.

Add or confirm the client details, select the service, confirm the branch, choose next available or preferred personnel, and place the client into the queue.

---

## 21.3 A client with an appointment arrives. What should I do?

Search for the appointment and check the client in.

Confirm the service, appointment time, branch, and personnel preference before moving the client into the active flow.

---

## 21.4 A client wants a specific personnel member. What should I do?

Select preferred personnel if the feature is enabled.

Show the client the extra fee and estimated wait time before confirmation. Record the selection clearly.

---

## 21.5 The preferred personnel is unavailable. What should I do?

Follow the branch workflow.

The client may wait longer, choose another eligible personnel, cancel, or require a fee reversal or invoice adjustment where applicable. Record the reason clearly.

---

## 21.6 A payment was recorded with the wrong reference. What should I do?

Do not ignore it.

Follow the branch correction workflow. If Finance validation is pending, notify Finance or request correction through the correct internal process.

---

## 21.7 The client says they paid, but the payment is not showing. What should I do?

Check the invoice, payment record, client, branch, and reference.

If no payment record exists, follow the branch workflow to record the payment or notify the appropriate user.

---

## 21.8 A receipt is not ready. What should I tell the client?

Check the payment status.

If the payment is pending Finance validation, explain that the payment details have been recorded and the receipt will be available after the payment reaches the correct validated state.

---

## 21.9 The queue looks wrong. What should I do?

Review the queue entries.

Check for duplicate records, cancelled clients, no-shows, wrong service selection, unavailable personnel, or preferred personnel delays. Make changes only where your role allows it and record reasons where required.

---

# 23. Security Basics

## 23.1 What should Front Office users do to keep access secure?

Front Office users should:

* Use their own email account.
* Never share Magic Links.
* Log out on shared devices.
* Keep devices secure.
* Avoid shared browsers where possible.
* Check that records belong to the correct branch.
* Avoid unnecessary downloads.
* Report suspicious activity quickly.
* Keep client and payment information confidential within the business.

---

## 23.2 Why is account sharing a problem?

Account sharing makes records unclear.

If several people use one Front Office account, the business cannot confidently tell who checked in a client, changed an appointment, assigned personnel, generated an invoice, or recorded payment details.

Each user should use their own account.

---

## 23.3 What should I do if I see suspicious activity?

You should:

1. Check the affected record.
2. Note the client, branch, time, and module.
3. Notify the Branch account user or Merchant Administrator.
4. Preserve helpful references where appropriate.
5. Use the Help & Support workflow if platform support is needed.

---

## 23.4 What should happen after Front Office access is suspended?

The user should no longer be able to log in.

Any active sessions and unused Magic Links should be invalidated. Historical records connected to the user should remain visible for accountability.
