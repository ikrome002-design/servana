# 1. About Servana

## 1.1 What is Servana?

Servana is a service-operations SaaS platform for African service-based SMEs.

It helps businesses manage clients, services, invoices, offline payment records, staff commissions, platform fees, permissions, and audit-ready records from one secure dashboard.

Servana is not only a booking app, POS system, or invoicing tool. It is a daily operating system for service businesses that need clearer records and better control.

No. Servana records offline or off-platform payments.

# 2. Merchant Audit Account Basics

## 2.1 What is the Merchant Audit account?

The Merchant Audit account is a read-only oversight account.

It allows authorized audit users to review important operational and financial activity without changing business records.

The role helps a merchant understand activity across the business, spot unusual events, review sensitive changes, and support internal accountability.

---

## 2.2 Who should use the Merchant Audit account?

The Merchant Audit account should be used by people trusted to review business activity carefully.

Typical users may include:

* Internal audit reviewers
* Business owners who want read-only oversight
* Senior managers
* Finance reviewers
* Operations reviewers
* Compliance or control reviewers inside the merchant business
* Trusted administrators assigned to review activity without editing records

This role should be assigned carefully because it can expose sensitive operational and financial information.

---

## 2.3 What is the main purpose of the Merchant Audit account?

The main purpose is to help the merchant answer practical questions such as:

* Who changed this role?
* Who validated this payment?
* Who rejected this payment?
* Who generated this receipt?
* Who voided this invoice?
* Who reassigned this queue entry?
* Who changed branch access?
* Who attempted to access a restricted record?
* Who exported a report?
* Which high-risk events need review?
* Which activity looks unusual?

The role supports visibility, trust, and accountability.

---

## 2.4 Is the Merchant Audit account an editing role?

No. The Merchant Audit account is a read-only role.

It should not create, edit, delete, validate, reject, approve, void, reassign, or update operational records.

It is designed for review, not action-taking.

---

## 2.5 Why is the Merchant Audit account read-only?

Read-only access protects the quality of audit review.

A user who reviews activity should not also be able to change the same records being reviewed. This separation helps keep oversight clean, reliable, and easier to trust.

---

## 2.6 What makes the Merchant Audit account different from the Merchant Administrator account?

The Merchant Administrator manages the merchant account at a high level.

The Merchant Audit account reviews activity without changing records.

The Merchant Administrator may manage branches, view performance, manage merchant-level setup, and oversee platform fee records. The Merchant Audit user focuses on activity logs, sensitive changes, financial history, role changes, suspicious activity, and audit review.

---

## 2.7 What makes the Merchant Audit account different from the Merchant Finance account?

The Merchant Finance account handles finance activity such as payment validation, receipts, disputes, refunds, reconciliation, and financial reports.

The Merchant Audit account reviews finance-related activity after it happens. It can see who recorded, validated, rejected, edited, voided, generated, or exported finance-related records, but it should not perform the finance action itself.

---

## 2.8 What makes the Merchant Audit account different from the Branch account?

The Branch account manages daily branch operations.

The Merchant Audit account reviews branch activity. It may see queue changes, appointment changes, service-session activity, branch status changes, and branch-level audit logs, but it should not operate the branch.

# 3. Access and Login

## 3.1 How does a Merchant Audit user log in?

Merchant Audit users log in through a Magic Link sent to their email address.

The user requests access, receives a login link, and uses that link to enter Servana.

---

## 3.2 Can a Merchant Audit user log in just because their email exists?

No. Servana checks more than the email address.

Before access is allowed, the platform checks that:

* The email belongs to an invited or active user.
* The user belongs to the correct merchant tenant.
* The account is active.
* The Merchant Audit role is active.
* The user has not been suspended.
* The user has the correct branch or merchant scope where scope applies.
* The Magic Link is valid, unused, and not expired.

This protects merchant records from accidental or unauthorized access.

---

## 3.3 What should I do when my Magic Link does not work?

Request a new Magic Link first.

A link may fail because:

* It has expired.
* It was already used.
* It was opened from a different browser or device.
* The wrong email inbox was checked.
* The account is inactive.
* The role is inactive.
* The user was suspended.
* The browser blocked the login flow.

If the issue continues, contact support through the approved Help & Support workflow.

---

## 3.4 Can Merchant Audit users share Magic Links?

No. Magic Links should not be shared.

Each user should access Servana through their own account. Sharing login links makes activity records unreliable and weakens security.

---

## 3.5 Can a Merchant Audit user be assigned to one branch only?

Yes. Access can be limited by branch where the merchant’s setup requires it.

A branch-scoped Audit user should only review activity for the assigned branch. A merchant-level Audit user may have broader visibility across multiple branches, depending on permissions.

---

## 3.6 What should happen when a Merchant Audit user leaves the business?

The user’s access should be removed, suspended, or deactivated promptly.

Historical records connected to that user should remain visible for accountability. Removing access should stop future login, but it should not erase past activity.

## 4.3 What are high-risk events?

Examples include:

* Role changes
* Finance permission changes
* Branch access changes
* Payment validation changes
* Payment rejection

* Receipt reissue

Recent activity is a chronological view of important events that happened inside the merchant account.

---

## 4.5 What are flagged items?

Flagged items are events that have been marked for review.

---

The Audit user may be able to mark a review status where the platform allows it, but the role should not change the original business record.

No. Merchant Audit users should not create client records.

Client creation belongs to the appropriate operational role, usually Front Office.

---

## 6.2 Can Merchant Audit users edit client records?

No. Merchant Audit users should not edit client records.

They may review related activity where permitted, but they should not change the record.

---

## 6.3 Can Merchant Audit users create or edit services?

No. Service creation and pricing belong to the Branch account user.

The Audit user may review service changes but should not create, edit, archive, or price services.

---

## 6.4 Can Merchant Audit users create invoices?

No. Merchant Audit users should not create invoices.

They may review invoice history and invoice-related audit events.

---

## 6.5 Can Merchant Audit users void invoices?

No. Invoice voiding should be handled by the appropriate authorized role and workflow.

The Audit user may review who voided the invoice, when it was voided, and why it was voided.

---

## 6.6 Can Merchant Audit users record payments?

No. Merchant Audit users should not record payments.

Payment recording belongs to the appropriate operational or finance workflow.

---

## 6.7 Can Merchant Audit users validate or reject payments?

No. Payment validation and rejection belong to the Finance workflow.

The Audit user reviews validation activity but should not perform the validation.

---

## 6.8 Can Merchant Audit users generate receipts?

No. Receipt generation should happen only through the appropriate finance or receipt workflow.

The Audit user may review receipt generation logs.

---

## 6.9 Can Merchant Audit users create or edit staff users?

No. Staff setup is handled by the appropriate HR or administrator role.

The Audit user can review staff access changes but should not create, edit, suspend, reactivate, or delete users.

---

## 6.10 Can Merchant Audit users change permissions?

No. The Audit role should not change user permissions.

It can review permission changes made by other authorized users.

---

## 6.11 Can Merchant Audit users manage queues?

No. Queue management belongs to Branch or Front Office users.

The Audit user can review queue changes, reassignments, cancellations, and no-show activity.

## 6.12 Can Merchant Audit users edit commissions?

# 7. Audit Logs

## 7.1 What is an audit log?

An audit log is a record of important activity inside Servana.

It helps show what happened, who did it, when it happened, where it happened, and which record was affected.

---

## 7.2 Why are audit logs important?

Audit logs help merchants protect trust inside the business.

They are useful for:

* Reviewing staff activity
* Investigating payment questions
* Understanding invoice changes
* Checking role changes
* Reviewing branch activity
* Following queue changes
* Reviewing suspicious access attempts
* Understanding report exports
* Supporting internal reviews

---

## 7.3 What should an audit log include?

A useful audit log should include:

* Date and time
* Actor
* Actor role
* Action performed
* Affected merchant
* Affected branch
* Affected module
* Affected record
* Previous value, where relevant
* New value, where relevant
* Severity
* Status
* Reason or note, where required
* Technical context, where available

---

## 7.4 What are previous and new values?

Previous and new values show what changed.

Example:

* Previous role: Front Office
* New role: Finance

This helps the Audit user understand the exact change instead of only seeing that “a role was updated.”

---

## 7.5 Why do sensitive changes need previous and new values?

Sensitive changes affect trust, access, money, or records.

Without previous and new values, the Audit user may know that something changed but not understand what changed.

This makes investigations slower and weaker.

---

## 7.6 Can audit logs be edited?

Audit logs should not be editable by merchant users.

The point of an audit log is to preserve a reliable record of activity. Editing audit logs would weaken trust in the system.

---

## 7.7 Can audit logs be deleted by Merchant Audit users?

No. Merchant Audit users should not delete audit logs.

Deleting audit records would remove important business history.

---

## 7.8 What does append-only mean?

Append-only means new activity can be added to the log, but existing audit records are not casually changed or removed.

This helps preserve the story of what happened.

---

## 7.9 What is tamper detection?

Tamper detection helps show whether audit records may have been changed in an unexpected way.

Servana may use technical methods such as hashes or chained hashes to support stronger audit integrity.

---

# 9. Severity Levels

## 9.1 What are severity levels?

Severity levels help show how important an event is.

They help the Audit user prioritize what needs review.

---

## 9.2 What severity levels may be used?

Servana may use:

* Info
* Low
* Medium
* High
* Critical

---

## 9.3 What is an Info event?

An Info event is normal activity that is useful for recordkeeping.

Examples may include:

* Standard login
* Normal appointment creation
* Standard queue update
* Routine branch activity

---

## 9.4 What is a Low event?

A Low event is usually routine but may still be useful during review.

Examples may include:

* Minor profile update
* Non-sensitive setting change
* Standard search activity

---

## 9.5 What is a Medium event?

A Medium event deserves more attention.

Examples may include:

* Service price update
* Appointment cancellation
* Queue reassignment
* Staff availability change
* Client profile update

---

## 9.6 What is a High event?

A High event affects important business records, access, money, or customer trust.

Examples may include:

* Role change
* Branch access change
* Payment validation
* Payment rejection
* Invoice void
* Receipt generation
* Report export
* Preferred personnel override

---

## 9.7 What is a Critical event?

A Critical event needs urgent review.

Examples may include:

* Unauthorized access attempt
* Suspicious export activity
* Duplicate payment reference override
* Financial period reopening
* High-risk permission grant
* Repeated failed access attempts
* Serious payment manipulation pattern
* Contact export attempt by an unexpected role

---

## 9.8 Should every Critical event be investigated?

Yes. Critical events should be reviewed promptly.

A Critical event does not always mean wrongdoing happened, but it does mean the event is important enough to check.

# 10. Role and Permission Review

## 10.1 Why should Audit users review role changes?

Roles control what users can see and do.

A role change can give someone access to clients, payments, invoices, reports, exports, staff records, or finance workflows. That makes role changes important to review.

---

## 10.2 What role changes should be watched closely?

Watch for changes involving:

* Finance access
* Audit access
* Administrator access
* Export access
* Branch access
* Payment validation permissions
* Receipt permissions
* Void permissions
* Refund permissions
* Period lock or reopen permissions

---

## 10.3 What should a role change log show?

A role change log should show:

* User affected
* Previous role
* New role
* Actor who made the change
* Date and time
* Branch affected
* Reason, where required
* Approval status, where configured

---

## 10.4 What is self-escalation?

Self-escalation happens when a user gives themselves more access or a higher-risk role.

Servana should prevent this wherever possible.

Audit users should treat self-escalation attempts as high-risk events.

---

## 10.5 What should Audit users do when a role change looks wrong?

The Audit user should:

1. Check the affected user.
2. Review who made the change.
3. Check the branch and role scope.
4. Review activity after the change.
5. Flag the event.
6. Notify the Merchant Administrator or Authorized Support Contact.
7. Use the Help & Support workflow where platform assistance is needed.

---

## 14.5 What receipt activity should be treated as high risk?

* Receipt downloaded unusually often

Preferred personnel waiting allows a client to wait for a specific service provider instead of taking the next available personnel.

For example, a client may prefer a specific barber, stylist, therapist, beautician, or massage therapist.

Flagging an event means marking it for attention.

An Audit user should flag an event when it is unusual, unclear, sensitive, or important.

* Repeated receipt reissue
* Duplicate payment reference override
* Queue override without reason

Good notes reduce confusion later.

# 20. Security Basics

## 20.1 What should Merchant Audit users do to keep access secure?

Merchant Audit users should:

* Use their own email account.
* Never share Magic Links.
* Keep their email account secure.
* Lock their devices.
* Avoid using shared browsers.
* Log out on shared devices.
* Report suspicious activity quickly.
* Avoid downloading records unnecessarily.
* Keep audit information confidential within the business.

---

## 20.2 Why is account sharing a problem?

Account sharing makes audit records unclear.

If several people use one account, the business cannot confidently tell who performed a review, export, flag, or other action.

Each user should have their own account.

---

## 20.3 What should Audit users do when they see suspicious access?

They should:

1. Review the event details.
2. Check the actor, branch, module, and timestamp.
3. Look for related activity.
4. Flag the event.
5. Notify the Merchant Administrator or Authorized Support Contact.
6. Use the Help & Support workflow where platform support is needed.

---

## 20.4 What should Audit users do after suspected email compromise?

They should notify the Merchant Administrator or internal account owner immediately.

The business should review:

* Login activity
* Recent exports
* Role changes
* Payment activity
* Receipt activity
* Branch access changes
* Suspicious review notes
* Any unusual activity after the suspected compromise

---

## 20.5 Can Audit users help detect internal misuse?

Yes. The Merchant Audit role is designed to help detect unusual patterns.

Examples include:

* Repeated payment edits
* Frequent invoice voids
* Many receipt reissues
* Unusual exports
* Role changes outside normal process
* Queue overrides without reasons
* Access attempts outside assigned branch
* Unexplained contact access attempts

---

# 21. Working With Other Roles

## 21.1 How should Audit users work with the Merchant Administrator?

The Audit user should share clear observations with the Merchant Administrator when an issue needs business attention.

For example:

* “This Finance user validated three payments without references.”
* “This branch had five receipt reissues today.”
* “This role change gave export access to a new user.”
* “This queue override happened without a reason.”

Clear facts help the Merchant Administrator act responsibly.

---

## 21.2 How should Audit users work with Finance?

Audit users should review Finance activity without taking over Finance work.

Finance handles validation, rejection, receipts, disputes, cash-up, refunds, and financial review. Audit checks whether the activity looks clear, consistent, and properly recorded.

---

## 21.3 How should Audit users work with HR?

Audit users may review HR activity such as role assignment, branch assignment, staff suspension, staff deactivation, and service eligibility changes.

HR manages staff records. Audit reviews the activity trail.

---

## 21.4 How should Audit users work with Branch users?

Branch users manage daily operations.

Audit users may review branch actions such as queue changes, appointment changes, service activity, day opening, day closing, branch status changes, and branch reports.

---

## 21.5 How should Audit users communicate concerns?

Audit users should communicate in a factual and calm way.

A good audit note says what happened, when it happened, who was involved, and what needs review.

Avoid vague statements such as “This looks bad.” Instead, write: “Payment record PAY-1021 was edited after validation by user A at 8:42 PM. Please review the reason for the edit.”
